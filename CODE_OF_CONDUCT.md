# Community conduct

Public discussion should support rigorous disagreement and accessible
participation. Criticism of the framework, founder, methods, evidence, and
institutional choices is welcome when it addresses relevant claims and actions.

Participants should distinguish evidence from interpretation, represent others'
arguments fairly, identify uncertainty, and correct material errors. Avoid
harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, deliberate misrepresentation,
publication of private information, and repetitive disruption. Do not pressure
participants to endorse the framework or disclose personal experiences.

Maintainers may request edits, limit disruptive participation, or remove harmful
content. Where feasible, explain the reason and give an opportunity to correct
conduct. Moderation should address behavior rather than suppress a substantive
objection. Relevant dissent should remain in the research record even when
harmful presentation is removed. Moderation does not create constitutional
jurisdiction or a constitutional remedy.

For an ordinary public conduct concern, use a repository issue without including
private information. For a sensitive concern, request a private reporting channel
with a minimal notification and wait for it to be established before sharing
details. A dedicated confidential conduct-reporting address is not specified
here. Maintainers should disclose conflicts and seek another reviewer when
available; this file does not invent an independent enforcement team or promise
confidentiality, resolution, or a response deadline.

These rules concern participation in the hosted community. They add no
copyright-use restriction to material licensed under CC BY 4.0.
