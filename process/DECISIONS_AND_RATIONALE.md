# Decisions made, reasons, and retained tradeoffs

**EXPLANATORY SUMMARY — ORIGINAL DECISION RECORDS CONTROL**

This account identifies recorded human design choices and explains their
rationale from the alternatives, advantages, failure modes, and review fields
in the source records. The explanations are a synthesis of those records, not
verbatim statements of the decision-maker's personal motives. A decision about
a proposed constitutional design is not ratification or operational authority.

## The five foundational decisions

### CDR-001 — Human sovereignty with binding constitutional constraint

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_HYBRID`, 2026-08-28: reserved human sovereignty over
foundational ends plus entrenched constitutional limits on ordinary power.
Source: [CDR-001](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDR-001.yaml).

The comparison considered an unconstrained foundational sovereign, a
constitution-bound sovereign without external adjudication, independent review,
distributed foundational authority, and the selected hybrid. Unconstrained
command was clear and fast but left arbitrary human power and capture poorly
checked. Self-constraint depended on the implicated authority judging its own
limits. Independent review strengthened contestation but risked reviewer
supremacy. Distribution reduced single-point capture while introducing deadlock
and informal recentralization.

The selected combination kept the source of foundational ends human while
binding ordinary human and artificial power. It incorporated independent human
review, appropriate distributed counter-power, independent verification,
publication, reason-giving, durable dissent, precedent and supersession, and
procedural regularity. Competence, popularity, reliance, and indispensability
were rejected as sources of authority.

**Retained cost:** the hybrid adds complexity and can become rigid or only
nominally human-controlled. Its review institutions still require their own
legitimate selection and constraint. It generated CR-001 through CR-006.

### CDR-002 — Prior emergency delegation with a narrow human-only fallback

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_E`, 2026-08-28.
Source: [CDR-002](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDR-002.yaml).

The alternatives were no emergency exception, narrow necessity, predelegation,
distributed concurrence, and a hybrid. An absolute prohibition strongly blocked
usurpation but could require catastrophic inaction. General necessity addressed
unforeseen harm but let an actor judge its own exception. Predelegation made
authority prospective and bounded, but could miss unforeseen threats.
Concurrence constrained unilateral action at the cost of delay and coalition risk.

The chosen design made explicit prior human authorization the default, with a
human-only necessity fallback under conjunctive conditions. Minimum protective
action, automatic expiry, no self-renewal, no precedent, durable records, and
prompt independent retrospective review were incorporated. AI could not invoke
necessity to enlarge its own authority. Success could not retroactively create
legitimacy, and a harmful outcome alone could not settle justification.

**Retained cost:** actors can manufacture unreachable-authority conditions or
expand fallback practice. Exact proof, eligibility, limits, and review needed
later design. It generated CR-007 through CR-013.

### CDR-003 — Continuity without sovereign succession

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_E`, 2026-08-28.
Source: [CDR-003](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDR-003.yaml).

The comparison covered one designated successor, a succession council,
continuity without sovereign succession, distributed interregnum authority,
and a hybrid. A single successor was legible but concentrated fraudulent-transfer
risk. A council could assess disputes but become self-perpetuating. Pure
continuity blocked automatic sovereignty but risked indefinite administration
or paralysis. Distributed authority reduced unilateral takeover but could deadlock.

The selected approach allowed only valid existing scoped grants and ordinary
offices to continue within their limits while foundational powers suspended.
A bounded human succession process, plural independent evidence, challenge,
dissent, uncertainty, anti-usurpation controls, and automatic expiry were
incorporated. Restoration could not depend on a continuity custodian's consent.
If lawful succession proved impossible, the exit was explicit refounding, not
machine inheritance or sovereignty through practical control.

**Retained cost:** administrators can entrench effective power during delay.
Proof standards, selectors, surviving grants, and outer limits required later
resolution. It generated CR-014 through CR-020.

### CDR-004 — Independent human judgment with constrained reviewers

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_E`, 2026-08-28.
Source: [CDR-004](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDR-004.yaml).

The alternatives were self-adjudication, independent human review, distributed
adjudication, a temporary special tribunal, and a hybrid. Self-adjudication
preserved source clarity but left self-interest insufficiently constrained.
Standing review provided capacity and continuity while concentrating interpretive
power. Distributed and special-conflict arrangements addressed capture at the
cost of coordination, consistent precedent, and selection complexity.

The chosen design used standing independent human adjudication, bounded appeal,
and predefined conflict handling. Judgments could bind ordinary constitutional
acts within granted jurisdiction. Appointment, tenure, removal, recusal,
replacement, reasons, dissent, provenance, and remedies had to be bounded.
Adjudication, enforcement, and verification were separated. Final machine
judgment, reviewer selection of foundational ends, and amendment or refounding
by interpretation were excluded.

**Retained cost:** review can still become effective sovereignty through evidence,
precedent, or remedies. A human signature cannot cure machine domination.
It generated CR-021 through CR-029.

### CDR-005 — Tiered change with an explicit refounding boundary

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_E`, 2026-08-29.
Source: [CDR-005](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDR-005.yaml).

The alternatives included full amendability, a permanently entrenched core,
tiered amendment, a refounding boundary, and a hybrid. Full amendability offered
correction but permitted identity replacement through ordinary procedures.
Permanent entrenchment protected a core but created rigidity and powerful
interpreters. Tiered amendment increased scrutiny without necessarily exposing
identity replacement. A binary refounding boundary lacked graduated treatment
for significant changes that preserved identity.

The selected arrangement distinguished ordinary amendment, structural amendment,
and explicit human refounding with acknowledged discontinuity. Ten foundational
principles were protected at the refounding level without being declared
metaphysically immutable. Cumulative changes, interpretation, and implementation
could not launder foundational replacement through smaller acts. Prior text,
reasons, dissent, and supersession history had to survive change.

**Retained cost:** classification itself creates contested power; demanding
change procedures can preserve defects. Reviewers may classify but cannot
supply the authorization. It generated CR-030 through CR-035.

## Coordinated completion of the architecture

### CDD-001 — Resolve interacting design questions together

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_ALL`, 2026-08-30.
Source: [CDD-001](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDD-001.yaml), the
[remaining-design matrix](../constitutional-design/REMAINING-DESIGN-MATRIX.yaml),
and [cross-domain packet](../constitutional-design/decisions/CROSS-DOMAIN-CONTRADICTION-DECISION-PACKET.md).

The five foundational decisions established directions but did not supply every
selector, threshold, lifecycle, or remedy. Eleven remaining design recommendations
were adopted as one coordinated package, subject to four corrections. Their
purpose was to prevent independently plausible components from contradicting
one another when combined. These summaries describe design topics; later
CDD-002 through CDD-004 and the current proposed text control refinements.

| Domain | Selected treatment in the coordinated work | Rationale and retained tension |
|---|---|---|
| RD-01 | Human membership, standing, and separated offices | Make the source and challenge paths explicit; inclusion rules can entrench incumbents and broad standing can overload review. |
| RD-02 | Distributed human court selection, bounded tenure, recusal and removal | Constrain appointments and capture; qualifications, selection, and tenure introduce their own power. |
| RD-03 | Bounded jurisdiction, appeal, finality, enforcement and remedies | Make review effective while preventing remedies from becoming appointment or sovereignty. |
| RD-04 | Emergency evidence, eligible actors, absolute prohibitions and expiry | Permit narrowly justified protection while resisting self-judged exceptions; proof and concurrence can delay action. |
| RD-05 | Prompt retrospective emergency review and act-specific remedies | Test exceptional action after urgency; missing evidence and irreversible harm complicate accountability. |
| RD-06 | Succession proof, surviving grants and plural human recognition | Resist fraudulent transfer while preserving bounded continuity; proof disputes can prolong interregnum. |
| RD-07 | Interregnum review, outer limit, return and restoration | Prevent temporary administration from becoming permanent; fixed limits may arrive before a dispute is resolved. |
| RD-08 | Distinct constituencies and procedures for amendment and refounding | Match authorization to consequence; higher thresholds and concurrence can obstruct correction. |
| RD-09 | Cumulative-effects and semantic-drift review | Expose gradual authority migration; aggregation can burden ordinary governance and enlarge classification power. |
| RD-10 | Accountable transition, obligations, assets, reliance and constitutional memory | Preserve people, claims, and history through discontinuity; custody can become practical sovereignty. |
| RD-11 | Dependencies, effective-power audits and substantive human control | Test real control rather than formal signatures; independence and diversification can be costly. |

The four cross-domain corrections addressed concrete interactions:

- **Initialization and vacancy:** bounded initial human appointments followed by
  separated processes prevented a circular bootstrap and denied vacancy-based
  authority to machines or incumbents.
- **Successor predesignation and failure fallback:** a valid prior selection,
  bounded recognition, and a human fallback avoided both a succession dead end
  and a selector inventing a successor.
- **Post-refounding accountability:** review, verification, enforcement, removal,
  and replacement had to be available when transition authority began, rather
  than being created by the custodian it was meant to constrain.
- **Reviewer remedies:** declaring invalidity or vacancy was separated from
  appointing replacements, creating offices, choosing ends, or ongoing management.

### Remainder sortition — Close a small rule with constitutional consequences

On 2026-08-30 the recorded `REMAINDER_SORTITION` instruction resolved unequal
structural-amendment cohort remainders. The largest equal cohorts were formed;
remaining eligible humans were assigned by auditable random sortition, not
human or machine discretion. The reason was to avoid a small administrative
choice becoming a means of manipulating concurrence. See
[OQ-001](../constitutional-design/drafts/CONSTITUTION_v0.1_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md).

### CDD-002 — Supply missing sources and selectors

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_B`, 2026-08-30.
Source: [CDD-002](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDD-002.yaml) and the
[blocker analysis](../constitutional-design/FINAL-RATIFICATION-BLOCKER-ANALYSIS.yaml).

The decision rooted the seven-human HCA College in constitutional membership,
with membership-selected successor alternates and Verification, separate
human-sortition selection of Enforcement, independent challenge, and a
structurally protected Schedule O selector class. The purpose was to close
missing authority chains without letting review, enforcement, vacancy, or
operational dependence manufacture their own selectors.

It did not finish every question. Post-incorporation review identified an
undecided College election result rule and removal lifecycle. The record
preserves that discovery rather than pretending Alternative B had resolved it.

### CDD-003 — Finish the College lifecycle without self-perpetuation

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_A`, 2026-08-31.
Source: [CDD-003](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDD-003.yaml) and the
[lifecycle analysis](../constitutional-design/HCA-COLLEGE-LIFECYCLE-ANALYSIS.yaml).

Direct fixed-membership approval elections, independent certification,
nonrenewal, no holdover, defined vacancy and incapacity triggers,
cause-bounded removal with constituent confirmation, and separately recognized
predesignated successors supplied the missing lifecycle. The reason was to keep
selection, certification, fact-finding, confirmation, review, recognition, and
enforcement from collapsing into one chain. Certification checked a human
result; it did not create authority. Review could declare a vacancy but could
not appoint the successor.

### CDD-004 — Define Schedule O, while refusing to invent its people

**Recorded choice:** `ADOPT_INITIAL_SCHEDULE_O_ARCHITECTURE`, 2026-08-31.
Source: [CDD-004](../constitutional-design/decisions/CDD-004.yaml) and
[Initial Schedule O](../constitutional-design/INITIAL_SCHEDULE_O.yaml).

Records Custodian, Financial Steward, and Operations Steward were defined as
three distinct ordinary human offices, with fixed three-year terms, alternates,
cause-based removal, materially independent membership-rooted selection, and
incompatibilities with constitutional and conflicting offices. They supplied
ordinary institutional anchors outside the principal constitutional chains.

Initial human authority could make initial appointments only; this did not
create a perpetual appointment power. The design intentionally left three
principals, three alternates, consent, and six attributable appointment records
unfilled. A fully specified office is not a person, and generated identities
cannot satisfy a human institutional commitment.

## What remains undecided or unperformed

The proposed text's closed design blockers do not mean that all research,
organic law, or implementation is complete. Schedule O population, attributable
ratification, activation, subordinate procedures, feasibility, and real-world
capture testing remain distinct. Historical entries preserve their evidence
limitations; empty dissent records do not imply unanimity. New explanatory
sections add no constitutional decision and cannot overwrite the source records.
