# Methodology: from historical questions to constitutional principles

**REPOSITORY-GROUNDED EXPLANATION — NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT**

Prepared 2026-10-03 from the authoritative research, analysis, and decision
records. This describes the documented process and offers a reading of its
logic. It does not invent a founder recollection, claim undocumented independent
review, or assert that all historical research was completed.

## 1. Begin with a problem of power

The starting problem was not how to make an AI obey a prompt. It was how an
institution survives when capability, reliance, infrastructure, and practical
control can accumulate independently of formal authority. The
[origin record](../history/ORIGIN.md) explains why a clean research repository
replaced accumulated experimental scaffolding: earlier experiments were not
allowed to become authoritative merely because they already existed.

The [working principles](../docs/DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md) served as research
orientation. A principle became a constitutional design commitment through
explicit human decisions and traced requirements, not simply by appearing in
an early list.

## 2. Use history to generate questions, not winners

The [historical register](../constitutional-design/sources/HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.yaml)
separates observation, interpretation, possible relevance, limitations, and
source status. Athens raised questions about participation and selection;
Rome about divided power, exceptional authority, and practical concentration;
Britain about convention, continuity, and changing executive control; and the
United States about written allocation, checks, review, and constitutional
change. Dutch, Swiss, and French/Burkean cases widened the comparison.

The discipline was to ask both what a mechanism protects and what it costs.
Rotation can disrupt entrenched control but reduce continuity. Independent
review can constrain abuse but acquire its own concentrated power. Distributed
authority can resist capture but become deadlocked or secretly recentralized.
Historical longevity, elegance, or fame did not settle these tradeoffs.

Specific historical connections are recorded in each CDR's `historical_analogues`
field. The accompanying historical essay adds limited source-supported context;
it does not substitute for the original evidence-status record.

## 3. Separate historical lessons from contemporary mechanisms

The [prior-art register](../constitutional-design/sources/PRIOR_ART_REGISTER.yaml)
and [mechanism map](../constitutional-design/sources/MECHANISM_ADOPTION_MAP.yaml)
considered existing institutional and technical mechanisms. They distinguished
candidate adoption, extension, design, and non-applicability. A policy check or
provenance record could be useful without answering who has legitimate authority
to set policy. Absence of a complete analogue was not treated as proof of novelty.

## 4. Form explicit questions and genuinely different alternatives

The eighteen inherited [issue records](../constitutional-design/issues/README.md)
were organized into five [foundational questions](../constitutional-design/FOUNDATIONAL_QUESTIONS.yaml).
Each foundational CDR compared five alternative architectures. The alternatives
included unconstrained authority, self-constraint, independent review,
distribution, predelegation, necessity, continuity, special tribunals,
entrenchment, and refounding, in different combinations.

This matters because a list of desirable principles can conceal contradictions.
Competing architectures expose choices: responsiveness versus capture risk,
independence versus coordination, flexibility versus identity preservation,
and continuity versus usurpation.

## 5. Review hostile humans as well as hostile machines

The records examine bad-human and bad-AI scenarios, incentives, continuity,
reversibility, human comprehensibility, and formal versus effective power.
They ask whether an actor can manipulate evidence, isolate the authorized human,
control appointments, exploit vacancy, prolong an emergency, capture a reviewer,
or use interpretation to change constitutional identity.

Assessments are qualitative, not a numerical optimization. The CDR reviews
explicitly reject rankings and automatic winners. Recorded attack analysis is
not evidence that the architecture was tested in a live institution. Where the
records say no dissent has been submitted, that means an empty dissent record,
not unanimity, external endorsement, or an absence of reasonable objections.

## 6. Require an explicit human decision

The human authority selected the foundational designs through recorded
instructions on 2026-08-28 and 2026-08-29. The CDRs identify the decision,
incorporated and rejected mechanisms, authorization record, date, and unresolved
questions. AI analysis, transcription, validation, and repository operations
were assigned support roles; they did not select constitutional ends or create
independent authority.

The decision-rationale account explains the selected tradeoffs using the
original analyses. It does not claim a more detailed personal motivation than
those records establish.

## 7. Translate decisions into requirements before drafting

The five foundational choices produced 35 accepted constitutional requirements,
then 20 consolidated groups. The traceability chain links sources, issues,
alternatives, review, human decisions, requirements, and provisions. This made
it possible to ask whether a sentence in the draft had a controlling basis,
whether an obligation was omitted, and whether an implementation choice had
been smuggled into constitutional law.

Cross-domain review then exposed bootstrap circularity, succession dead ends,
transition-accountability gaps, and remedies that could create reviewer
sovereignty. CDD-001 adopted coordinated resolutions. Later CDD-002 and
CDD-003 supplied missing authority allocations and a complete lifecycle;
CDD-004 defined the ordinary-office architecture needed for Schedule O.

## 8. Preserve correction rather than invent retrospective completeness

The repository retains earlier residual uncertainty and reviews alongside
later resolutions. An early document can correctly record an unresolved issue
that a subsequent decision closes. Historical records should not be edited to
pretend the answer was known from the beginning. Later summaries must identify
the relevant decision and avoid presenting every earlier recommendation as
adopted.

## 9. Keep drafting, ratification, activation, and implementation separate

Constitution v0.1 is proposed text. Its text-level review and repository tests
cannot supply the six Schedule O people, consent, appointment records, human
ratification, or later activation conditions. A maintained public repository is
not an operating constitutional institution.

The development sequence therefore reserves Phase H for subordinate governance
law, Phase I for formal representation, and Phase J for validation and
enforcement after the necessary normative work. This methodology publishes
institutional reasoning; it discloses no private cognitive-engine design.

## What the method establishes—and what it does not

It establishes an inspectable chain of recorded design choices, rejected
alternatives, rationale, and traceability. It does not establish empirical
superiority, historical inevitability, legal enforceability, universal
transferability, or immunity to capture. Implementation feasibility, operating
cost, constitutional performance, and broader historical verification remain
subjects for further work.

## Later extension: leadership as an institutional comparison

The [five leadership studies](../research/leadership/README.md), added 2026-10-04, pair selected achievements with failures or limits in Eisenhower, Wilson, Churchill, Disraeli and FDR. They separate primary statements, institutional historical context, present constitutional interpretation and unexecuted evaluation questions. The [worksheet](LEADERSHIP_REVIEW.md) is a proposed practice aid. These cases are later learning, not retroactive evidence for the original choices; personal reputation and favorable outcomes do not substitute for authorization, inclusion or delivered remedies.

## Later extension: cross-disciplinary comparison

The [sixteen additional studies](../research/cross-disciplinary/README.md), added 2026-10-04, examine political leadership, philosophy, economics and social science. They use purposive selection, competing considerations and explicit distinctions among inspected primary text, scholarly summary, publisher overview and indexed excerpts. Interpretation supplies proposed questions about participation, local knowledge, human feasibility and systemic correction. This is neither a completed literature review nor retroactive evidence for original design choices.

## Deepening source review without rewriting provenance

The [targeted source ledger](../research/cross-disciplinary/SOURCE_REVIEW.md), added later on 2026-10-04, records accessible primary passages for six priority contributors. Earlier indexed-excerpt limitations remain in public Git history; current notes state their improved but still bounded source coverage. The [synthesis](../research/cross-disciplinary/DEEP_SYNTHESIS.md) separates existing constitutional commitments, proposed subordinate practices and normative questions that require actual human decisions. Its evaluation scenarios remain unexecuted.

## Later extension: decision-making and empirical evaluation

The [business and econometrics review](../research/decision-making/README.md),
added 2026-10-04, selects eleven works for framing choices, examining evidence,
learning and evaluating effects. Its source register distinguishes inspected
primary passages, publisher abstracts and practitioner descriptions, retaining
access and version limits. Inclusion recommendations are supporting practice,
not retrospective origins or new accepted constitutional requirements.

The [unified workflow](DECISION_MAKING.md) connects objectives, alternatives,
uncertainty and accountable action to the existing review architecture. The
[evaluation supplement](../testing/programs/DECISION_EVALUATION.md) keeps
descriptive observations, identified causal effects and model predictions
separate. Measuring a desirable effect cannot settle authority or contested
ends. No operational observation or comparative efficacy follows from preparing
these documents.
