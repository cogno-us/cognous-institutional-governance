# History: origins, influences, and recorded development

**PROJECT HISTORY — NOT AN ADOPTED CONSTITUTION**

Alvorada emerged from experiments in governed AI collaboration, institutional
reasoning, and constitutional design. The [origin record](ORIGIN.md) explains
why a clean repository was created: accumulated scaffolding and conversation
state made provenance and authority boundaries harder to preserve. Earlier
experiments were not automatically inherited as authoritative law.

The account here documents the repository's recorded development. It does not
claim to reconstruct every earlier conversation or assign dates to events for
which the repository supplies none.

## Intellectual and historical influences

The [comparative constitutional study](COMPARATIVE_CONSTITUTIONAL_HISTORY.md)
explains the incorporation of Greek and Roman institutional history, British
parliamentary development, and the American constitutional experiment. The
existing research also considers Dutch and Swiss experience, Burke, the French
Revolution, and cross-cutting constitutional lessons.

The comparison asked which mechanisms constrain power, how they fail, and what
they cost. Historical evidence informed alternatives and adversarial questions;
explicit human design decisions selected Alvorada's commitments. The historical
register remains preliminary. Newly reviewed sources support specific passages
in the study and do not silently upgrade that entire register.

## Preserving history without granting it authority

Older analytical records retain unresolved questions that later decisions may
resolve. That is provenance, not necessarily a current contradiction. Conversely,
a historical experiment, recommendation, successful test, or publication cannot
be promoted into an appointment, ratification, or operational delegation.

Prior public commits are retained; this history does not claim to retract
previous disclosures. The present framework remains proposed constitutional
research awaiting real human institutional acts.
