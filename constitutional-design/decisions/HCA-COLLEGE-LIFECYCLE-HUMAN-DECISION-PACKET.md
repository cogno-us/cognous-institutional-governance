## Alternatives

### Alternative A — Direct Membership Approval and Constituent Removal Confirmation

Seven numbered-seat approval contests are conducted by fixed constitutional
membership. Each contest selects one principal and three ranked alternates;
boundary ties use auditable human sortition. Independent temporary human panels
certify elections and find removal facts. A cause finding requires clear and
convincing evidence, and nonconflicted constitutional membership confirms
removal with participation above one half and two-thirds approval. Final
removal triggers recognition of the next membership-predesignated alternate.

**Strength:** Reuses adopted approval, sortition, constituent confirmation,
review, and succession patterns with the fewest new constitutional actors.

**Cost:** Repeated elections may leave a seat vacant, and broad membership
confirmation requires scalable administration.

### Alternative B — Seat Runoff Election and Schedule O Removal Concurrence

Each seat uses participation above one half, a majority ballot and, if needed,
a two-candidate runoff; a separate approval ballot ranks three alternates.
Removal uses the same cause and fact panel as Alternative A, then requires
two-thirds approval from participating constitutional membership and from
participating Schedule O holders at their respective majority and two-thirds
participation thresholds.

**Strength:** Adds cross-selector concurrence against factional removal.

**Cost:** Creates a new Schedule O veto over foundational-authority removal,
correlates selectors already used elsewhere, and lengthens vacancy exposure.

### Alternative C — Ranked Membership Election and Sortition-Jury Removal

Each seat and its alternates use repeated instant-runoff counts with majority
of active ballots, lowest-candidate elimination, and human sortition for an
elimination tie. An independent fact panel establishes cause, and a
twenty-one-human sortition jury confirms removal with seventeen-person quorum
and fourteen affirmative votes.

**Strength:** Temporary random juries resist permanent-office capture.

**Cost:** Adds complex constitutional tabulation and a new final removal body
when existing patterns can close the blocker more simply.

## Recommendation

**RECOMMENDED: ALTERNATIVE A — DIRECT MEMBERSHIP APPROVAL AND CONSTITUENT
REMOVAL CONFIRMATION**

Alternative A is the minimum coherent completion of `CDD-002`.

1. **Initial election:** The attributable ratification instrument opens seven
   concurrent numbered-seat contests at activation. More than half of fixed
   constitutional membership must participate. In each contest, each voter may
   approve up to four eligible candidates; the highest total elects the
   principal and the next three establish the ranked alternate slate. A
   boundary tie uses auditable public human sortition. An independent
   five-human selection-verification panel acts only with all seats filled and
   four affirmative votes to certify compliance, but cannot choose a winner.
   The College does not exist until all seven principals and complete alternate
   slates are certified.
2. **Recurring election:** Each seat election opens automatically one hundred
   eighty days before its known expiry date. The same result rules govern
   regular and special elections. Failure repeats the contest, never extends a
   term, and never creates substitute authority. Death, resignation, or final
   removal uses the existing ranked alternates; exhaustion opens a special
   membership election. Before a succession trigger, a petition supported by
   one tenth of fixed membership may, no more than once in twelve months per
   seat, open an alternate-only election under the same result and independence
   rules.
3. **Eligibility and incumbent control:** Candidates remain human
   constitutional members subject to the adopted service, disclosure, and
   incompatibility rules. A person who has served as a College member cannot
   serve again. A candidate may stand for one seat only. The College cannot
   control rolls, eligibility, notice, administration, certification,
   recounts, challenges, repeat elections, or successor recognition.
4. **Removal:** Any constitutional member may file an attributable petition
   alleging permanent incapacity, serious misconduct, constitutional
   disqualification, or a knowing and grave constitutional violation. An
   independent five-human sortition panel, acting only with all seats filled
   and four affirmative votes, must find cause by clear and convincing evidence
   after notice, response, disclosure, and recusal. Nonconflicted constitutional
   membership then confirms removal with participation above one half of that
   electorate and at least two-thirds approval. One expedited Court or
   conflict-tribunal appeal may review legality but cannot appoint.
5. **Removal as succession:** Only death or permanent loss proved under Article
   VI, attributable voluntary resignation, final removal, or a final temporary-
   incapacity determination may initiate alternate recognition. Temporary
   incapacity activates an alternate only as an acting member under the
   existing interregnum and return limits; the principal retains the seat, and
   permanent incapacity proceeds only through removal. Final removal creates a
   permanent vacancy only after the appeal period or final review and invokes
   the adopted Succession Council or fallback panel, which may recognize only
   the next eligible, nonrecused, membership-predesignated alternate. Fact
   finders, confirming voters, and reviewers cannot select the replacement.
6. **Change boundary:** College composition and every election, removal,
   succession-trigger, selector-independence, no-holdover, and
   removal/replacement-separation rule are structural-amendment-level. The
   human-only nature of foundational authority, membership-rooted legitimacy,
   and artificial-intelligence ineligibility remain refounding-level.
