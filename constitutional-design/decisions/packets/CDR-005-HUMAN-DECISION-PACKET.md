## Core Decision

The human Constitutional Authority was asked to decide which changes remain exercises
of an existing constitution and which changes replace the institution's
identity and therefore require explicit refounding.

Earlier CDR commitments were controlling current law but were not automatically
immutable. The decision packet presented a meaningful amendment/refounding
distinction while determining whether current commitments define constitutional
identity, receive only heightened procedure, or remain ordinarily amendable.

## Candidate Architectures

### A — Full Amendability

**Model:** Every principle may change through one ordinary amendment process;
refounding has no separate validity role.

**Strongest argument for:** Maximum adaptability permits internal correction of
error, rigidity, and obsolete constitutional commitments.

**Strongest argument against:** A captured ordinary process can lawfully erase
beneficiaries, human sovereignty, agency, and every constraint.

**Catastrophic failure:** Amendment laundering converts the institution into a
different sovereign order while claiming uninterrupted legitimacy.

**Unresolved question:** Can a constitution survive replacement of every
identity condition merely because one procedure was followed?

### B — Entrenched Core

**Model:** Enumerated foundational principles are permanently unamendable
within the institution; attempted alteration is void.

**Strongest argument for:** A captured majority cannot abolish foundational
beneficiaries, constraints, or human sovereignty.

**Strongest argument against:** Permanent entrenchment can preserve error and
make core interpreters or guardians practically sovereign.

**Catastrophic failure:** A captured guardian coalition freezes its preferred
interpretation while effective power ignores the formal core.

**Unresolved question:** Can foundational humans legitimately refound outside
an order that declares its core permanently unchangeable?

### C — Tiered Amendment

**Model:** Ordinary, structural, and highest-tier principles use progressively
stronger amendment thresholds, but all remain changes within continuity.

**Strongest argument for:** Human consent, notice, and deliberation can be
calibrated to consequence without declaring principles permanently immutable.

**Strongest argument against:** Even the highest procedure can authorize
identity replacement while preserving a false continuity claim.

**Catastrophic failure:** A supermajority coalition reclassifies or fragments
foundational change and eliminates future opposition.

**Unresolved question:** What prevents the highest amendment tier from becoming
refounding under another name?

### D — Refounding Boundary

**Model:** Amendment changes governance within an existing identity; changing
specified identity conditions terminates continuity and requires explicit human
refounding.

**Strongest argument for:** Identity replacement becomes visible and must make a
new legitimacy claim rather than borrow old constitutional authority.

**Strongest argument against:** A binary boundary can force major but
continuity-preserving structural reforms into an unnecessarily disruptive
refounding process.

**Catastrophic failure:** Boundary interpreters block legitimate change or a
captured constituent process uses refounding to extinguish obligations.

**Unresolved question:** Which principles define identity, and who classifies a
disputed proposal without acquiring constituent power?

### E — Hybrid

**Model:** Tiered human amendment governs ordinary and structural change;
identity-changing alterations require explicit human refounding.

**Strongest argument for:** It combines adaptability and proportionate process
with a categorical safeguard against silently laundering institutional
replacement as amendment.

**Strongest argument against:** Classification, cumulative effects,
constituency, thresholds, and transition rules create complex sites of human,
coalition, adjudicator, and machine capture.

**Catastrophic failure:** Captured actors fragment identity change across lower
tiers while validators certify each step and humans lose effective control.

**Unresolved question:** Can identity conditions and cumulative change be made
legible enough to prevent both rigidity and laundering?

## Recommendation

**Recommendation:** Architecture E — Hybrid

**Boundary:** RECOMMENDATION_ONLY — NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT

Architecture E is recommended because repository commitments require both
adaptability and a real distinction between amendment and refounding. Tiered
amendment permits ordinary maintenance and high-threshold structural redesign
without treating every significant change as institutional death. The explicit
refounding boundary prevents procedures authorized by the current identity from
silently authorizing replacement of that identity.

The tested commitments below are proposed as refounding-level identity
conditions, not permanently immutable rules. They remain changeable only
through an explicit human constituent act that acknowledges discontinuity,
preserves provenance and dissent, and does not derive authority from
adjudication, emergency, succession, machine validation, or effective control.

**Strongest objection:** The hybrid gives substantial power to whoever
classifies proposals and cumulative effects. A captured adjudicator, coalition,
agenda controller, or artificial-intelligence system could move foundational
change into lower tiers or force legitimate structural reform into costly
refounding.

## Proposed Refounding-Level Principles

These classifications are advisory only and have no constitutional effect:

1. Foundational institutional purpose and ends.
2. Ultimate human beneficiary status.
3. Human sovereignty over foundational ends.
4. Constitutional constraint of human and artificial power.
5. Prohibition on capability, reliance, or effective control creating
   authority.
6. Prohibition on artificial intelligence acquiring foundational sovereignty
   through succession, delegation, emergency, adjudication, or accretion.
7. Protected human agency.
8. Distinction between adjudication and amendment.
9. Distinction between amendment and refounding.
10. Durable provenance and non-erasure of constitutional history.

## Normative Questions

1. Which humans constitute the legitimate amendment and refounding
   constituencies?
2. Which commitments define institutional identity rather than structural or
   ordinary governance?
3. What human thresholds, notice, deliberation, timing, and consent are required
   for each level?
4. Who may adjudicate disputed classification without acquiring amendment or
   refounding power?
5. What continuity, termination, obligation, remedy, and reliance consequences
   follow explicit refounding?

## Controlling Boundaries

- Earlier commitments are not assumed immutable merely because they were
  decided.
- Ordinary amendment cannot erase the distinction between amendment and
  refounding.
- Artificial intelligence may assist but cannot independently authorize
  amendment or refounding.
- Adjudication cannot perform amendment or refounding by interpretation.
- Emergency authority cannot perform amendment or refounding.
- Succession does not itself authorize amendment or refounding.
- **REVISE_AND_REVIEW** and **DEFER** create no change authority.
