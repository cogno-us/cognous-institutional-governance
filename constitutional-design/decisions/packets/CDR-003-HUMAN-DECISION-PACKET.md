## Core Decision

The human Constitutional Authority must decide how legitimate human
foundational authority can resume after temporary incapacity, permanent loss,
or a disputed succession without allowing continuity actors to manufacture
sovereignty.

CDR-001 reserves foundational sovereignty to legitimate humans. CDR-002 permits
only a human necessity fallback that is minimal, expiring, nonprecedential, and
incapable of amendment, refounding, or sovereignty transfer. Neither
administrative continuity nor emergency success can decide FQ-03.

## Candidate Architectures

### A — Single Designated Successor

**Model:** One predesignated human succeeds on a valid trigger, with temporary
acting authority distinguished from permanent succession.

**Strongest argument for:** Fast, legible transfer minimizes interregnum and
creates one human authority chain.

**Strongest argument against:** Capture, forgery, or manipulation of one
designation and trigger can transfer the entire institution.

**Catastrophic failure:** A successor manufactures incapacity or uses forged
evidence to obtain irreversible foundational control.

**Unresolved question:** Who independently determines disputed incapacity,
designation, identity, revocation, and return?

### B — Succession Council

**Model:** A predefined human body determines succession or temporarily
exercises bounded authority through plural evidence and judgment.

**Strongest argument for:** Multiple humans can contest evidence and reduce
single-person succession capture.

**Strongest argument against:** Appointment, quorum, agenda, and evidence
control can turn the council into a self-perpetuating sovereign.

**Catastrophic failure:** A captured coalition validates its preferred
successor and suppresses restoration or challenge.

**Unresolved question:** How can the council bind a dispute without acquiring
foundational sovereignty over the succession process?

### C — Continuity Without Sovereign Succession

**Model:** Existing valid delegations and ordinary offices continue narrowly,
but no actor gains foundational sovereignty until legitimate human succession
or refounding.

**Strongest argument for:** It most clearly separates administrative continuity
from constitutional legitimacy.

**Strongest argument against:** Long vacancy can create paralysis or transfer
effective sovereignty to administrators, operators, and artificial intelligence.

**Catastrophic failure:** Indefinite continuity becomes permanent government by
formally subordinate but operationally indispensable actors.

**Unresolved question:** What legitimate process ends the vacancy before
bounded continuity becomes a de facto constitutional order?

### D — Distributed Interregnum Authority

**Model:** Several independent human offices receive limited temporary scopes
and concurrence requirements, while none inherits foundational sovereignty.

**Strongest argument for:** Distribution reduces unilateral usurpation and
preserves independent evidence, dissent, and operational redundancy.

**Strongest argument against:** Deadlock, coalition capture, and shared
infrastructure can defeat both responsiveness and genuine independence.

**Catastrophic failure:** A shared artificial intelligence or coordination hub
controls every nominally independent office.

**Unresolved question:** Which scopes, concurrence rules, and time bypasses
preserve continuity without aggregating into sovereignty?

### E — Hybrid

**Model:** Continuity without sovereign succession is the default, combined
with a bounded human succession process and distributed anti-usurpation
controls.

**Strongest argument for:** It preserves neutral administration while providing
a human path out of indefinite interregnum without automatic sovereign transfer.

**Strongest argument against:** Complexity multiplies proof, standing,
coordination, review, and capture disputes while operational actors entrench.

**Catastrophic failure:** A procedurally valid-looking human process is captured
while continuity infrastructure makes its preferred outcome unavoidable.

**Unresolved question:** What process is legitimate enough to recognize a
successor yet bounded enough not to become the new foundational sovereign?

## Recommendation

**Recommendation:** Architecture E — Hybrid

**Boundary:** RECOMMENDATION_ONLY — NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT

Architecture E best preserves the controlling decisions. Its default rule
follows Architecture C: valid ordinary grants may preserve narrow, reversible
administration, but continuity does not confer foundational sovereignty. It
then adds a bounded human succession process so restraint does not harden into
indefinite vacancy, paralysis, or effective takeover.

Distributed evidence, control, and challenge reduce single-channel capture.
The succession result must remain human, while machines may only validate
limited formal properties such as credentials, provenance, grants, clocks, and
concurrence. CDR-002 emergency authority stays separate and cannot become a
succession trigger, office, precedent, or sovereignty transfer.

**Strongest objection:** The hybrid may create so many proof, standing,
concurrence, review, and restoration disputes that the bounded process cannot
produce a timely legitimate result. During delay, administrators or artificial
intelligence systems may acquire effective sovereignty even though every formal
rule denies it.

## Normative Questions

1. Who may determine temporary incapacity, permanent loss, death, return,
   identity, and standing, and under what challenge standard?
2. Which existing offices and delegations survive, suspend, or expire during
   interregnum?
3. What human process may recognize a successor without itself becoming the
   holder of foundational sovereignty?
4. How long may bounded continuity persist before succession or refounding is
   required?
5. What restoration, remedy, and reliance rules apply after a mistaken,
   manipulated, or disputed transfer?

## Consequences and Boundaries

- **A** requires exact designation, trigger, proof, acting, revocation, and
  restoration rules.
- **B** requires council membership, standing, quorum, recusal, appeal,
  independence, and anti-entrenchment rules.
- **C** requires a precise inventory of surviving grants, suspended powers,
  duration, and a legitimate exit from indefinite vacancy.
- **D** requires separated scopes, concurrence, evidence independence, deadlock,
  restoration, and shared-infrastructure controls.
- **E** requires all continuity boundaries plus a bounded human succession
  process that cannot aggregate temporary authority into sovereignty.
- **REVISE_AND_REVIEW** and **DEFER** create no succession authority.

No option allows artificial intelligence to inherit or acquire foundational
sovereignty through continuity, capability, delegation, necessity, performance,
reliance, or indispensability. No option converts CDR-002 emergency authority
into succession. No machine-valid record or administrative operation establishes
constitutional legitimacy.

## Material Ambiguities

- Proof and adjudication of incapacity, permanent loss, death, return,
  identity, and standing.
- Which grants survive and which foundational or irreversible powers suspend.
- Legitimate composition and authority of a bounded human succession process.
- Maximum duration of continuity and the succession-versus-refounding boundary.
- Restoration, invalidation, remedy, and reliance after disputed acts.
- Resistance to forged evidence, manufactured incapacity, coalition capture,
  shared artificial intelligence dependence, and effective-power takeover.
