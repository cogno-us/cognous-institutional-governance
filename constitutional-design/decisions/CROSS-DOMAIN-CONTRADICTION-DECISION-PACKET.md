## Component 1 — Human-Only Initialization and Vacancy

**Affected domains:** `RD-01`, `RD-02`, `RD-03`, `RD-11`

**Affected consolidated requirements:** `CCR-003`, `CCR-013`, `CCR-014`,
`CCR-015`, `CCR-016`

**Affected source requirements:** `CR-004`, `CR-006`, `CR-021`, `CR-022`,
`CR-023`, `CR-024`, `CR-025`, `CR-026`, `CR-027`, `CR-028`, `CR-029`

**Decision:** At initial constitutional activation, the Human Constitutional
Authority may make only the minimum appointments necessary to initialize the
Constitutional Court, Verification Office, Enforcement Office, and any
constitutionally required conflict-tribunal mechanism. This authority is
bootstrap authority only. It expires for each institution when that institution
can use its ordinary constitutionally separated human appointment process and
expires entirely when all listed institutions are initialized.

After initialization, appointments, vacancies, removals, and replacements must
use constitutionally separated human processes. They may not remain dependent
on unilateral foundational appointment. No artificial-intelligence system may
appoint, remove, replace, or become the final selector of a constitutional
officeholder.

Every temporary vacancy mechanism must:

- use predefined human eligibility and selection rules;
- preserve function without expanding jurisdiction;
- expire when the ordinary authorized selection process succeeds;
- create no precedent or new appointment authority; and
- provide a human challenge path that does not depend on the vacant,
  conflicted, or not-yet-initialized institution.

The Constitution may assign detailed eligibility administration, nomination,
sortition, scheduling, and vacancy processing to subordinate governance law,
but that law cannot change the human-only selectors, separation rules,
jurisdiction, expiry, or challenge boundary.

**Contradiction removed on adoption:** The Court, verifier, enforcer, and
conflict mechanism no longer depend circularly on one another for initial
existence, and no validator or artificial-intelligence operator becomes the
final gatekeeper of its creator.

## Component 2 — Successor Predesignation and Failure Fallback

**Affected domains:** `RD-04`, `RD-06`, `RD-07`, `RD-11`

**Affected consolidated requirements:** `CCR-006`, `CCR-008`, `CCR-010`,
`CCR-012`

**Affected source requirements:** `CR-008`, `CR-009`, `CR-010`, `CR-012`,
`CR-013`, `CR-017`, `CR-018`, `CR-020`

**Decision:** Foundational human authority must maintain one or more
constitutionally valid successor designations. A designation is valid only
when made, attributed, reviewable, and kept current through the human process
authorized by the Constitution; it remains revocable through that same process
before a succession trigger.

If no valid designated successor is available, the human succession process
adopted under `CDR-003` determines succession. If that process cannot achieve
its required quorum after ordinary alternates and recusals are exhausted, a
predefined fifteen-human fallback panel activates.

Before any succession trigger, thirty constitutional members must be placed in
a public ranked fallback pool by auditable human sortition. Pool eligibility
uses the Succession Council's human independence and conflict rules and excludes
Council members, continuity custodians, known successor claimants, emergency
actors, and operators of materially dependent evidence or
artificial-intelligence systems. The first fifteen eligible, nonrecused persons
in rank order form the panel; recusals and vacancies are filled in order.
Twelve members constitute quorum and ten affirmative votes determine a
succession trigger or recognize a successor. The panel must apply the plural
evidence and proof burdens adopted for the ordinary succession process, publish
attributable reasons and dissent, and remain subject to one expedited
Constitutional Court appeal. Neither the sortition administrator nor any
machine system has discretion to alter the rank or select the successor.

No vacancy, quorum failure, operational necessity, emergency,
artificial-intelligence recommendation, or effective control creates
foundational sovereignty. If neither the ordinary nor fallback human process
can make a constitutionally valid determination by day 150 of the interregnum,
the panel must record failure and issue public notice initiating access to the
`RD-08` explicit refounding process. This notice does not authorize refounding.
Continuity remains bounded by `RD-07`: it ends at the 180-day maximum, and every
surviving grant expires at its original expiry or that ceiling, whichever is
earlier. Failure to complete succession or refounding by the ceiling creates no
continuing, residual, emergency, or sovereign authority.

Subordinate governance law may administer designation records, periodic
confirmation, evidence intake, alternate pools, scheduling, and recounts. It
cannot alter who may hold foundational authority, the adopted proof burdens,
the thirty-person ranked pool, fifteen-person panel, twelve-person quorum,
ten-vote threshold, independence and review requirements, or the
succession/refounding and 180-day expiry boundaries.

**Contradiction removed on adoption:** Succession has a valid designation
lifecycle and a human, non-custodial non-quorum path without converting
continuity, emergency, machine recommendation, or practical control into
sovereignty.

## Component 3 — Accountable Post-Refounding Transition

**Affected domains:** `RD-01`, `RD-03`, `RD-08`, `RD-10`, `RD-11`

**Affected consolidated requirements:** `CCR-003`, `CCR-013`, `CCR-015`,
`CCR-017`, `CCR-018`, `CCR-019`, `CCR-020`

**Affected source requirements:** `CR-004`, `CR-006`, `CR-021`, `CR-022`,
`CR-026`, `CR-027`, `CR-030`, `CR-031`, `CR-032`, `CR-033`, `CR-034`,
`CR-035`

**Decision:** A refounding act may establish a temporary human-governed
Transition Authority solely to execute the transition explicitly authorized by
that act. The act must enumerate the Authority's powers and identify the
legitimate human institutions that will independently review and verify its
conduct, enforce controlling decisions against it, and remove and replace its
members. Those institutions and their challenge path must be constituted and
capable of acting when the refounding takes effect. A refounding design lacking
that accountability chain is constitutionally incomplete and cannot take
effect.

The Transition Authority:

- cannot alter the new foundational ends;
- cannot amend or refound;
- cannot self-renew;
- cannot create permanent offices unless the refounding act explicitly
  authorizes that creation;
- cannot appoint or control its own reviewers, verifiers, enforcers, removers,
  or replacements;
- remains subject to independent human review, verification, enforcement,
  removal, and replacement; and
- automatically expires on the constitutional condition or outer limit stated
  in the refounding act.

Expiration cannot enlarge continuity authority. If the new order is not fully
operative at expiration, only authority independently and explicitly granted
by the refounding act survives; the Transition Authority gains no residual,
emergency, or successor power.

Subordinate governance law may provide transition reporting, claims handling,
asset inventories, record transfer, notice, scheduling, and replacement
administration. It cannot create or extend transition authority, alter
foundational ends, weaken independent accountability, or erase prior
constitutional history.

**Contradiction removed on adoption:** Refounding cannot activate an
unreviewable custodian. Review, enforcement, removal, replacement, and expiry
are conditions of a complete transition design rather than powers inferred
after constitutional discontinuity.

## Component 4 — Reviewer-Non-Sovereign Remedies

**Affected domains:** `RD-01`, `RD-03`, `RD-09`, `RD-11`

**Affected consolidated requirements:** `CCR-003`, `CCR-013`, `CCR-014`,
`CCR-015`, `CCR-016`, `CCR-019`

**Affected source requirements:** `CR-004`, `CR-006`, `CR-021`, `CR-022`,
`CR-023`, `CR-024`, `CR-025`, `CR-026`, `CR-027`, `CR-028`, `CR-029`,
`CR-034`

**Decision:** The Constitutional Court may determine that an appointment,
officeholder, selection process, dependency structure, or exercise of
authority violates the Constitution. It may:

- invalidate an unlawful appointment;
- suspend unlawful authority;
- declare a vacancy;
- order the separately authorized constitutional selection process to occur;
- require disclosure, verification, recusal, independence safeguards, or
  narrowly bounded diversification necessary to remedy the demonstrated
  violation; and
- restore lawful constitutional state.

The Court may not:

- appoint a replacement unless an explicit constitutional rule separately
  grants a narrowly defined emergency selection function;
- create a constitutional office;
- redesign institutional ends;
- create foundational authority;
- create or transfer amendment or refounding authority;
- exercise continuing managerial control; or
- impose open-ended diversification or restructuring.

Replacement is ordinarily performed by a separately authorized human selector.
Any expressly granted emergency selection function must identify its human
selector, trigger, eligibility rules, scope, challenge path, and nonrenewable
expiry, and cannot enlarge Court jurisdiction or create precedent.

A diversification remedy must be tied to a demonstrated constitutional
violation; necessary, proportionate, and specific; independently reviewable;
and time-bounded where appropriate. It cannot appoint personnel, create or
reallocate constitutional offices, determine institutional ends, control
ordinary management, or alter amendment or refounding constituencies,
thresholds, or authority.

Subordinate governance law may define evidence formats, compliance milestones,
reporting intervals, procurement alternatives, and verification methods. It
cannot enlarge the remedy set, transfer appointment power to a reviewer, or
convert a bounded remedy into institutional management.

**Contradiction removed on adoption:** Vacancy declarations and diversification
orders cannot become judicial appointment, restructuring, amendment, or
continuing management powers.
