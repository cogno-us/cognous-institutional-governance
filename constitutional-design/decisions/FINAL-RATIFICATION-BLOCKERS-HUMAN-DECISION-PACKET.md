## Decision Scope

One package must decide:

1. who constitutes Human Constitutional Authority after activation and how
   constitutional membership supplies its legitimacy;
2. who creates, maintains, revokes, and recognizes valid successor
   designations;
3. the continuing selectors, removal confirmation, alternates, replacement,
   and independent challenge paths for Verification and Enforcement; and
4. the exact constitutional boundary, creation source, and anti-manipulation
   protection of the ordinary-human-governance-office selector class.

The components are interdependent and should not be severed. A selector cannot
be evaluated independently of the body it selects, the reviewer of that
selection, and the succession or vacancy path that follows failure.

## Recommended Integrated Architecture

### 1. Human Constitutional Authority

After constitutional activation, Human Constitutional Authority consists of a
seven-human HCA College elected by the fixed constitutional membership.
Membership is the constituent human root; the College is its bounded
post-activation institutional holder.

- College members serve seven-year nonrenewable terms.
- A member must satisfy the human, disclosure, independence, incompatibility,
  and anti-machine-domination conditions in the analysis.
- A foundational act requires six College votes and direct membership
  confirmation with participation of at least two thirds and approval by at
  least three quarters of valid votes.
- Neither the College acting alone nor membership acting outside a
  constitutionally authorized process may create foundational authority.
- Office, performance, reliance, emergency, continuity, vacancy, or effective
  control creates no HCA status or additional authority.

Changing human sovereignty over foundational ends, the membership-rooted source
of HCA legitimacy, or artificial-intelligence ineligibility for foundational
authority requires explicit refounding.

### 2. Successor Designation

Constitutional membership creates valid successor designations by electing
three public ranked alternates for each HCA College seat through the same
human election that selects that seat.

- The College maintains and publishes the designation record as a ministerial
  duty. It cannot choose, reorder, veto, or make its own successor effective.
- Membership may revoke or replace an alternate through the same election
  process before a succession trigger.
- The adopted Succession Council, or the adopted fallback panel when
  applicable, recognizes the next eligible nonrecused alternate under the
  existing proof, independence, reason, dissent, challenge, and appeal rules.
- Recognition confirms satisfaction of the Constitution; it does not select or
  create foundational authority.
- Exhaustion invokes a new membership election and creates no interim,
  emergency, continuity, machine, or effective-control authority.
- Existing day-150 notice, explicit-refounding access, and 180-day ceiling
  remain unchanged.

### 3. Verification Office

The Verification Office has three human members serving staggered six-year
nonrenewable terms.

- The fixed constitutional membership selects them with participation above
  one half, approval voting for up to three candidates, the three highest
  totals prevailing, and auditable human sortition resolving a final-seat tie.
- The same process selects three ranked alternates.
- Removal requires a five-human independent sortition fact panel to find
  incapacity, serious misconduct, or constitutional disqualification by clear
  and convincing evidence, followed by confirmation from a twenty-one-human
  nonconflicted sortition jury with seventeen-person quorum and two-thirds
  approval of the full jury.
- A same-channel alternate fills the remainder of the term. Exhaustion repeats
  the ordinary membership selection process.

### 4. Enforcement Office

The Enforcement Office has three human members serving staggered six-year
nonrenewable terms.

- Public auditable human sortition selects them from the constitutionally
  qualified pool independently of the Court and Verification Office.
- The same sortition produces three ranked alternates.
- Removal uses the same independent fact panel and evidence burden, followed by
  fixed constitutional membership confirmation with participation above one
  half and majority approval of valid votes.
- A same-channel alternate fills the remainder of the term. Exhaustion repeats
  the ordinary sortition process.

### 5. Eligibility, Selection Verification, and Challenge

Verification and Enforcement candidates must be human constitutional members,
serve voluntarily, publish conflicts and material dependencies, satisfy a
public human-administered competence standard, and for three years have held no
HCA College, Court, opposite-office, succession-claimant, emergency-command,
continuity-custodian, institutional artificial-intelligence operation, or
material institutional contracting role.

A five-human ad hoc panel selected by public human sortition from
noncandidate, nonofficeholding constitutional members verifies compliance with
the selection process. Neither permanent office verifies its own selection.

Any constitutional member may bring one expedited Court challenge. Whole-Court
conflict invokes the adopted conflict tribunal. The Court may invalidate an
unlawful process, declare a vacancy, and order the authorized process to occur,
but may not appoint, remove directly, create an office, change a selector, or
exercise continuing management.

### 6. Ordinary Human Governance Offices

Only human-held ordinary governance offices exhaustively identified in a
constitutional **Schedule O** qualify for the ordinary-office selector channel.
Each Schedule O entry must state:

- the office identifier;
- its ordinary, nonconstitutional jurisdiction;
- its human selector;
- its term;
- its alternates;
- its removal path; and
- its incompatibilities.

Constitutional adjudication, Verification, Enforcement, HCA College,
succession, transition, emergency, continuity, artificial-intelligence
operation, and candidate roles cannot qualify.

The Human Constitutional Authority adopting the Constitution approves the
initial Schedule O as part of ratification. Ratification is incomplete unless
the schedule contains at least three separately held, materially independent
qualifying offices. Later entry, deletion, merger, division, or
reclassification requires structural amendment.

Ordinary governance law may create and administer ordinary offices, but an
office does not enter Schedule O by function, title, delegation, practice, or
effective control. Renaming, temporary assignment, delegation, or functional
transfer does not change schedule status.

The Schedule O electorate is fixed before public notice of each constitutional
exercise. Each nonconflicted human holder has one vote; holding multiple listed
offices does not multiply voting power. Any constitutional member may challenge
eligibility or conflict status through one expedited Court appeal. The Court
may enforce but not amend the schedule.

### 7. Change and Non-Accretion Boundaries

Structural amendment is required to change:

- HCA College composition, term, vote, or membership-confirmation rule;
- Verification or Enforcement composition, selector, term, removal,
  replacement, or challenge path; or
- any Schedule O entry, qualification, vote, or anti-manipulation protection.

Explicit refounding is required to change:

- human sovereignty over foundational institutional ends;
- the constitutional membership as the human source of HCA legitimacy; or
- artificial-intelligence ineligibility for foundational sovereignty.

Ordinary amendment, ordinary law, adjudication, emergency, succession,
continuity, implementation, precedent, vacancy, acquiescence, capability,
reliance, and effective control cannot waive or alter these allocations.

## Subordinate Governance and Implementation

If the recommendation is adopted, subordinate governance law may regulate only:

- nomination, election, notice, ballot, recount, and publication mechanics;
- sortition entropy, ceremony, reproducibility, audit, and interfaces;
- conflict and dependency disclosure formats and evidence intake;
- competence-assessment administration under the constitutional standard;
- alternate records, term calendars, vacancy notices, and acceptance of
  service;
- Schedule O registries and incumbent-roll administration without
  classification power;
- successor record registration, confirmation, revocation records, and
  notification;
- expedited case administration, record retention, and reporting; and
- identity, authentication, signatures, storage, archival integrity, and
  accessibility tooling.

Subordinate law cannot create HCA authority, select or recognize a successor,
change a constitutional selector, alter eligibility or thresholds, classify an
office into Schedule O, remove an officeholder outside the constitutional
process, or turn a verifier, reviewer, machine, or operator into a gatekeeper of
constitutional legitimacy.
