# Governed agency: Phase H design requirements

**PROPOSED SUBORDINATE DESIGN — NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT**

Date: 2026-10-03. Provenance: André de Lima requested repository incorporation
of the governed-agent, delegation-expansion, human-agency, and capture-analysis
recommendations. This records a requested design workstream, not adoption of
organic law, a constitutional decision, ratification, or operational delegation.
The supplied conversation is the source of these proposals; no claim is made

Constitution v0.1 remains the controlling proposed text. Its design need not
be reopened merely because these supporting proposals are recorded. A genuine

## Authority topology and the governed agent

Constitution I.1(22–25), III.1(2), and IV.1–IV.2 separate cognitive capability,
delegated agency, effective power, and constitutional authority. A governed
agent may reason, recommend, plan, adapt, and execute within valid delegation.
It cannot originate foundational authority, convert competence into
jurisdiction, or become the exclusive path through which humans exercise
constitutional authority or remedies.

Affective, logical, and principle-based cognition are not compliance classes.
Emotion does not establish unauditability; logic does not guarantee truthful
records; possession of principles does not itself select institutional ends.
Audit the actual decision record, delegation, dependency, and human control.
Reconstruction concerns the material reasoning relied upon in a decision; it
must not be confused with a demand for complete machine introspection.

## H-GA-01 — Delegation expansion and authority lifecycle

**Trace:** I.1(22–25); IV.1(1,3–4); IV.2(2,6); VII.3.

Proposed rule: any material expansion of delegated actions, affected resource
classes, side effects, authority domains, beneficiary populations, or execution
consequences requires new attributable authorization from the competent human
authority before the expansion takes effect. Capability acquisition, technical
availability, prior success, inferred intent, reliance, or ordinary operational
necessity cannot supply that authorization.

Design the lifecycle as proposal, human authorization, bounded operation,
review, renewal or modification, and suspension, revocation, or expiry. Each
grant should identify its human source, eligible holder, scope, constraints,
duration, review and revocation paths, and restoration conditions. Record old
and new scopes, reasons, alternatives, dependencies, uncertainty, and correction
history. Neither the recipient nor its validator may approve its own expansion.

Review cumulative changes and material dependency shifts against IV.2 and
VII.3. Organic law cannot choose new constitutional selectors, thresholds,
change categories, or foundational ends. Emergency cases remain governed by
Article V, including its narrowly specified human-only necessity fallback;
this proposal neither broadens nor removes that constitutional exception.

Acceptance evidence for the later design:

- A read-and-summarize grant cannot silently become a modify-and-notify grant.
- A new tool or more capable model supplies no additional authority by itself.
- A scope comparison and competent human authorization precede expansion.
- Suspension, revocation, expiry, and correction preserve attributable history
  and do not leave residual authority with an agent or infrastructure operator.
- Related small changes receive cumulative review when VII.3 requires it.

## H-GA-02 — Practical human agency, replacement, and alternatives

**Trace:** IV.1; IV.2(1–9); VIII.1(1.g).

Design procedures that preserve relevant humans' practical capacity to
understand, deliberate, contest, choose, correct, coordinate, replace systems,
and withdraw delegation. Map these proposed operational requirements to the
competent human actors and existing constitutional review paths; they create
no new constitutional office, standing, or remedy.

For material decisions, require evidence that responsible humans can
independently understand the material basis, reconstruct the reasoning, reject
the recommendation, choose a viable nonmachine alternative, and control timing
and effect. A signature, vote, nominal override, or attractive dashboard alone
is insufficient. These are the existing IV.2 tests, not substitute definitions.

Specify practicable alternatives with access to records, adequate resources,
usable procedures, and time to act. A second AI is not a nonmachine alternative.
Document replacement and withdrawal exercises, including continuity of access
to records and remedies when a provider, model, credential service, or operator
is removed. Replacement must follow competent human authorization and existing
review paths; it cannot become an independent power to appoint officeholders.

Dependency disclosures should cover common control in evidence, data, models,
infrastructure, identity, communications, financing, agenda, enforcement, and
incentives. Preserve independent audit evidence and material dissent. Failure
to demonstrate human control calls for review and appropriate remedies through
Article III; this document does not itself issue an enforceable judgment.

Acceptance evidence for the later design:

- Humans exercise rejection, correction, timing control, and withdrawal in a
  realistic scenario rather than merely identifying a button.
- A viable nonmachine path survives loss of the principal AI service.
- No single model, issuer, evidence store, communications path, or operator
  gates a constitutional exercise or remedy.
- Replacement preserves records, dissent, and review access without transferring
  constitutional authority to the replacement provider.

## Implementation firewall and later Phase I representation

These are design inputs for proposed organic law. They do not authorize machine
encoding, a compiler, identity enrollment, voting software, validators, or
runtime enforcement. Complete subordinate procedures and their competent human
adoption before treating them as operative. Ratification and activation cannot
be inferred from a commit, model output, or passing test.

Any later formal representation must preserve the human-readable authority
boundaries, decision records, review paths, and human-control requirements
set out above. No data model, protocol, algorithm, or implementation schema is
published or adopted here. Validation may report conformity; it cannot ratify,
adjudicate, confer standing, or determine legitimacy.
