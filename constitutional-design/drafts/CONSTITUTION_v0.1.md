# Constitution of Alvorada — Proposed v0.1

**PROPOSED FOR HUMAN RATIFICATION — NOT YET ADOPTED**

This text is the complete constitutional proposal prepared from the decisions
and requirements identified in its traceability record. It has no
constitutional effect unless adopted by legitimate Human Constitutional
Authority through the applicable constitutional process.

## Article I — Constitutional Identity and Human Sovereignty

### Section I.1 — Reserved Human Sovereignty and Non-Accretion

1. Alvorada SHALL exist for foundational institutional purposes and ends
   determined by legitimate Human Constitutional Authority for the ultimate
   benefit of human beings.
2. Human Constitutional Authority SHALL remain sovereign over foundational
   institutional ends. In exercising institutional power, every human and every
   artificial system SHALL remain subject to this Constitution.
3. After constitutional activation, Human Constitutional Authority SHALL be
   exercised only by a College of seven human constitutional members elected by
   the fixed constitutional membership.
4. Constitutional membership SHALL be the constituent human root of the
   College's legitimacy. The College SHALL exercise only the bounded
   post-activation foundational authority granted by this Constitution.
5. Every candidate for a College seat or ranked alternate designation SHALL be
   a human constitutional member, accept service voluntarily, and disclose
   conflicts and material dependencies. A College member or ranked alternate
   SHALL NOT simultaneously hold Court, Verification, Enforcement, Succession
   Council, Transition Authority, continuity-custodian, emergency-command, or
   material institutional artificial-intelligence-operation status. Each
   College member SHALL serve a seven-year nonrenewable term, staggered where
   practicable.
6. A person who has served as a College member SHALL NOT again be elected or
   designated as an alternate. Alternate status without service SHALL NOT
   consume that limit. A candidate MAY stand for only one of the seven numbered
   College seats in an election. A candidate SHALL NOT administer or serve on
   an election, fact-finding, removal-confirmation-administration, or review
   body for the same exercise.
7. The attributable instrument ratifying this Constitution SHALL give public
   notice opening seven concurrent numbered-seat elections at constitutional
   activation. The fixed constitutional membership determined under Section
   II.2 immediately before notice SHALL be the electorate.
8. Participation SHALL exceed one half of the fixed constitutional membership.
   In each seat contest, each participating member MAY approve up to four
   eligible candidates. The highest approval total SHALL elect the principal,
   and the next three totals SHALL establish that seat's ranked alternate
   slate. A tie affecting the principal or alternate order SHALL be resolved
   only by auditable public human sortition among the tied candidates.
9. A five-human ad hoc selection-verification panel SHALL be selected by public
   human sortition from noncandidate, nonofficeholding constitutional members.
   With all five seats filled, four affirmative votes SHALL be required to
   certify compliance. The panel SHALL publish the count, sortition record,
   reasons, and dissent. Certification SHALL verify the authorized process and
   result and SHALL NOT choose a winner or create authority independently of
   the membership decision. Any constitutional member MAY bring one expedited
   validity challenge to the Court or applicable conflict tribunal, which MAY
   affirm, vacate, or require a rerun but SHALL NOT appoint.
10. The initial College SHALL exist only after all seven principals and three
    alternates for each seat have been certified. An incomplete seat contest
    SHALL repeat automatically. No founding actor, incumbent, reviewer,
    emergency actor, artificial-intelligence system, or effective controller
    MAY fill a seat or exercise College authority because an election is
    incomplete.
11. Each recurring seat election SHALL open automatically one hundred eighty
    days before the known expiry of its term. The electorate, approval,
    ranking, tie, certification, and challenge rules in paragraphs 7 through 9
    SHALL govern every regular and special election. A failed or incomplete
    contest SHALL repeat. At term expiry the seat SHALL become vacant; neither
    the incumbent nor the prior alternate slate SHALL hold over.
12. Death or permanent loss proved under Article VI, attributable voluntary
    resignation, or final removal SHALL invoke the ranked-alternate succession
    process for the remainder of the term. Alternate exhaustion SHALL open a
    special membership election automatically. Before a succession trigger, an
    attributable petition supported by at least one tenth of fixed
    constitutional membership MAY open an alternate-only election for a seat,
    no more than once in twelve months. The principal SHALL remain in office,
    and each participating member MAY approve up to three eligible candidates.
    The three highest approval totals SHALL establish the replacement alternate
    slate in rank order. Paragraph 7, the tie rule in paragraph 8, and the
    certification and challenge rules in paragraph 9 SHALL apply; no principal
    SHALL be selected or displaced in that exercise.
13. The College SHALL NOT control the membership roll, eligibility,
    nominations, notice, administration, certification, recount, challenge,
    repeat election, removal process, or successor recognition for a College
    seat.
14. A foundational act SHALL require six College votes and direct confirmation
   by the fixed constitutional membership, with participation of at least two
   thirds and approval by at least three quarters of valid votes.
15. Any constitutional member MAY file an attributable petition to remove a
    College member only for permanent incapacity, serious misconduct,
    constitutional disqualification, or a knowing and grave violation of this
    Constitution. The College as a body SHALL NOT initiate, block, terminate,
    or control the proceeding.
16. A five-human fact panel SHALL be selected by auditable public human
    sortition from noncandidate, nonofficeholding, nonconflicted constitutional
    members. With all five seats filled, four affirmative votes and clear and
    convincing evidence SHALL be required to find cause after notice and a
    meaningful opportunity to respond.
17. The fact panel SHALL publish the evidence, reasons, dissent, conflicts, and
    material dependencies. The subject, accuser, a materially interested
    person, a College member, a ranked alternate for the affected seat, or an
    operator of materially dependent evidence or artificial-intelligence
    systems SHALL NOT serve on the panel.
18. After a cause finding, nonconflicted fixed constitutional membership MAY
    confirm removal only with participation above one half of that
    nonconflicted electorate and approval by at least two thirds of valid
    votes. The subject SHALL NOT participate. Neither the fact panel nor the
    confirming electorate MAY select or reorder the replacement in that
    proceeding.
19. The subject or any constitutional member MAY take one expedited appeal to
    the Court or applicable conflict tribunal. Review MAY affirm, vacate, or
    remand for constitutional error but SHALL NOT appoint, rank, recognize, or
    veto a successor. Removal SHALL be ineffective until the appeal period
    expires without appeal or final review affirms it. A pending proceeding
    SHALL create no vacancy, holdover extension, or transfer of foundational
    authority.
20. Election administration, certification, removal fact-finding, constituent
    confirmation, legal review, succession recognition, replacement selection,
    verification, and enforcement SHALL remain institutionally separable. No
    actor exercising one function SHALL acquire another by necessity, vacancy,
    dependence, or effective control.
21. Neither College action alone nor membership action outside a process
   authorized by this Constitution SHALL create foundational authority.
22. Artificial intelligence MAY exercise autonomy only within authority validly
   delegated under this Constitution. Artificial intelligence SHALL NOT acquire
   foundational sovereignty, determine foundational institutional ends, or
   inherit Human Constitutional Authority.
23. Capability, competence, performance, reliability, popularity, validation,
   delegation, reliance, operational necessity, emergency, succession,
   adjudication, precedent, effective control, continuity, or operational
   indispensability SHALL NOT create, enlarge, transfer, or prove constitutional
   authority.
24. Formal authority and effective power SHALL remain distinct. The exercise of
   effective power without lawful authority SHALL NOT become legitimate through
   acquiescence, success, duration, dependence, or later description.
25. No succession, emergency, delegation, interpretation, cumulative practice,
   or technical validation MAY convert artificial or practical control into
   foundational sovereignty.

## Article II — Constitutional Authority, Constraint, and Separation of Functions

### Section II.1 — Binding Constitutional Constraint

1. This Constitution SHALL bind every ordinary human institution, officeholder,
   delegate, adjudicator, emergency actor, continuity custodian, transition
   custodian, and artificial system acting for or through Alvorada.
2. Human sovereignty over foundational ends SHALL NOT validate an ordinary act
   that violates this Constitution.
3. Constitutional review MAY bind ordinary institutional action only within the
   jurisdiction and remedies granted by this Constitution. Review SHALL NOT
   become sovereignty over foundational ends.
4. No institution MAY derive additional authority from its ability to interpret,
   administer, enforce, validate, or technically execute this Constitution.

### Section II.2 — Constitutional Membership and Standing

1. For each constitutional exercise, constitutional membership SHALL consist of
   every adult natural person who, immediately before the exercise's initiation
   event, was either subject to Alvorada's claimed authority or within its
   published class of ultimate human beneficiaries.
2. Artificial intelligence SHALL NOT be a constitutional member.
3. The initiation event SHALL be:
   a. filing of a constitutional proposal or case;
   b. publication of notice for an office-selection exercise;
   c. filing of a succession trigger;
   d. opening of transition nominations; or
   e. for any other constitutional exercise, public notice identifying its
      purpose before participation begins.
4. A public pre-exercise membership roll SHALL close before participation. The
   roll SHALL be rebuttable evidence of membership and SHALL NOT itself create or
   extinguish membership.
5. Inclusion or exclusion from the roll SHALL be reviewable before the exercise
   proceeds.
6. Standing SHALL exist for:
   a. a person alleging personal constitutional injury;
   b. a representative holding attributable authorization from represented
      constitutional members;
   c. a person bringing a public-interest challenge to a material institutional
      violation; and
   d. a constitutional office acting within its assigned function.
7. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate filing, proof, notice, and orderly
   administration of membership and standing. It SHALL NOT narrow the
   constitutional membership test, make a machine system the final gatekeeper,
   or eliminate a constitutionally protected standing channel.

### Section II.3 — Separated Functions, Initialization, and Vacancies

1. Constitutional authorization, adjudication, enforcement, evidence
   validation, independent verification, succession administration, and
   transition custody SHALL remain functionally separated.
2. No office SHALL combine final constitutional judgment with enforcement or
   control of the evidence on which that judgment depends. Verification SHALL
   remain independent from selection, adjudication, and enforcement.
3. At initial constitutional activation, Human Constitutional Authority MAY
   make only the minimum human appointments necessary to initialize the
   Constitutional Court, Verification Office, Enforcement Office, and any
   constitutionally required conflict-tribunal mechanism.
4. Bootstrap appointment authority SHALL expire for each institution when that
   institution can use its ordinary constitutionally separated human selection
   process, and SHALL expire entirely when all institutions named in paragraph 3
   have been initialized.
5. After initialization, appointments, vacancies, removals, and replacements
   SHALL follow constitutionally separated human processes and SHALL NOT remain
   dependent on unilateral foundational appointment.
6. An artificial-intelligence system SHALL NOT appoint, remove, replace, or
   become the final selector of a constitutional officeholder.
7. A temporary vacancy mechanism:
   a. SHALL use predefined human eligibility and selection rules;
   b. MAY preserve the assigned function but SHALL NOT expand jurisdiction;
   c. SHALL expire when the ordinary authorized selection process succeeds;
   d. SHALL create no precedent or new appointment authority; and
   e. SHALL provide a human challenge path independent of the vacant,
      conflicted, or not-yet-initialized institution.
8. Subordinate governance law MAY administer eligibility, nominations,
   sortition, scheduling, and vacancy processing only within these boundaries.
9. Artificial intelligence MAY assist administration, verification, sortition
   integrity, provenance, and analysis. It SHALL NOT vote in a constitutional
   exercise, hold a constitutional office, select, appoint, remove, or replace
   an officer, control selector eligibility or a sortition outcome, certify
   human constitutional legitimacy, or become a final constitutional
   adjudicator.

## Article III — Constitutional Adjudication and Enforcement

### Section III.1 — Constitutional Court and Jurisdiction

1. A Constitutional Court of nine human members SHALL exercise final
   constitutional judgment within the jurisdiction granted by this Article.
2. Artificial intelligence MAY assist research, evidence analysis, simulation,
   provenance, and formal validation, but SHALL NOT exercise final
   constitutional judgment.
3. The Court MAY adjudicate:
   a. the constitutionality of ordinary institutional acts;
   b. compliance with emergency-authority conditions and limits;
   c. succession, incapacity, identity, loss, and return determinations;
   d. constitutional-office eligibility, removal, and recusal;
   e. noncompliance with final constitutional judgments;
   f. formal and effective-power challenges; and
   g. classification of proposed constitutional change.
4. The Court SHALL NOT select foundational institutional ends, create
   constitutional authority, or create amendment, refounding, emergency, or
   succession authority.
5. Classification of a proposed constitutional change SHALL determine the
   applicable process only. It SHALL NOT authorize, enact, or supply the change.

### Section III.2 — Selection, Qualification, Terms, and Removal

1. Three Court seats SHALL be elected by the fixed constitutional membership.
   Participation SHALL exceed one half of that membership. Each participating
   member MAY approve up to three eligible candidates. The three candidates
   receiving the highest approval totals SHALL be elected. A tie for the final
   seat SHALL be resolved by public human sortition.
2. Three Court seats SHALL be elected by all nonconflicted holders of ordinary
   human governance offices. Participation SHALL be at least two thirds. The
   approval and tie rules in paragraph 1 SHALL apply.
3. Three Court seats SHALL be filled by public human sortition from the
   qualified pool.
4. The qualified pool SHALL consist only of constitutional members who:
   a. volunteer;
   b. publicly disclose conflicts and material dependencies;
   c. complete a public, human-administered constitutional competence review by
      the Verification Office, subject to one Court appeal; and
   d. have held no governance, enforcement, institutional
      artificial-intelligence-operation, or material institutional-contracting
      role during the preceding three years.
5. Each selection channel SHALL choose three ranked alternates by the same
   process used for its seats.
6. Court terms SHALL be nine years, nonrenewable, and staggered by thirds.
7. A Court member MAY be removed only for incapacity, serious misconduct, or
   constitutional disqualification.
8. Removal SHALL require:
   a. a finding by clear and convincing evidence from a five-human special
      removal panel drawn by human sortition from the qualified pool; and
   b. separate approval by both channels that did not appoint the member.
9. For removal approval:
   a. the membership-channel body SHALL be the full fixed constitutional
      membership, with participation exceeding one half and approval by a
      majority of valid votes;
   b. the governance-channel body SHALL be all nonconflicted ordinary human
      officeholders, with a two-thirds quorum and approval by a majority of all
      eligible officeholders; and
   c. the sortition-channel body SHALL be a twenty-one-human jury drawn from the
      qualified pool, with seventeen members constituting quorum and approval
      by two thirds of the full jury.
10. Abstentions SHALL count for quorum but not approval. Invalid ballots SHALL
    not count as approval. A tie SHALL fail.
11. If fewer than three eligible governance officeholders remain, or a material
    common conflict disables a channel body, a twenty-one-human jury drawn from
    nonconflicted constitutional members SHALL substitute under the quorum and
    approval rules in paragraph 9(c).
12. Only a human-held ordinary governance office exhaustively identified in
    constitutional Schedule O SHALL qualify for the ordinary-office selection
    channel.
13. Each Schedule O entry SHALL state the office identifier, ordinary
    nonconstitutional jurisdiction, human selector, term, alternates, removal
    path, and incompatibilities.
14. Court, Verification, Enforcement, HCA College, succession, transition,
    emergency, continuity, institutional artificial-intelligence-operation, and
    candidate roles SHALL NOT qualify for Schedule O.
15. The ratification instrument SHALL supply the initial Schedule O with at
    least three separately held and materially independent qualifying offices.
    The Constitution SHALL NOT become operational without that schedule.
16. A later addition, deletion, merger, division, or reclassification of a
    Schedule O entry SHALL require structural amendment. Ordinary governance
    law MAY create or administer an ordinary office but SHALL NOT give it
    Schedule O status or alter that status, selector, or constitutional weight.
17. Renaming, delegation, temporary assignment, practice, implementation,
    machine classification, acquiescence, or effective control SHALL NOT change
    Schedule O status.
18. The Schedule O electorate SHALL be fixed before public notice of each
    constitutional exercise. Each nonconflicted human holder SHALL have one
    vote; holding multiple listed offices SHALL NOT multiply voting power.
19. Any constitutional member MAY challenge Schedule O eligibility, selector
    composition, or conflict status through one expedited Court appeal. The
    Court MAY enforce Schedule O but SHALL NOT amend it.

### Section III.3 — Recusal, Panels, Appeal, and Conflict Tribunal

1. A judge SHALL recuse for a personal, financial, appointing-party,
   prior-participation, or material-dependency conflict.
2. A recusal or vacancy SHALL be filled from the same channel's ranked
   alternates.
3. Ordinary first-instance cases SHALL be decided by three-human panels.
   Appeals SHALL be decided by five-human panels containing no member of the
   first-instance panel.
4. One appeal SHALL produce finality. A final judgment MAY be reopened only for
   fraud, concealed conflict, or decisive evidence that was previously
   unavailable.
5. If the whole Court is compromised, a temporary nine-human conflict tribunal
   SHALL be constituted. Each Court-selection channel SHALL use its ordinary
   process to choose three nonconflicted members and three ranked alternates.
   Current or former judges and parties to the case SHALL be ineligible.
6. Conflict-tribunal recusals SHALL be filled by alternates from the same
   channel. Six members SHALL constitute quorum, and six affirmative votes SHALL
   be required for judgment.
7. A conflict-tribunal judgment SHALL be final, subject only to the reopening
   grounds in paragraph 4. The tribunal SHALL dissolve after judgment.

### Section III.4 — Remedies, Enforcement, Verification, and Reviewer Limits

1. To remedy a proven constitutional violation, the Court MAY issue a stay,
   invalidate or suspend unlawful authority, remand an act, require disclosure,
   restore lawful constitutional state, order restitution, or grant
   reliance-protective prospective relief.
2. Every remedy SHALL be necessary, proportionate, specific, and confined to the
   Court's jurisdiction.
3. A separate human Enforcement Office SHALL execute final constitutional
   judgments. An independent Verification Office SHALL report publicly on
   compliance.
4. Each Office SHALL have three human members serving staggered six-year
   nonrenewable terms and three ranked same-channel alternates.
5. The fixed constitutional membership SHALL select Verification members with
   participation exceeding one half. Each participating member MAY approve up
   to three eligible candidates; the three highest totals SHALL prevail, and
   public human sortition SHALL resolve a final-seat tie.
6. Enforcement members and their ranked alternates SHALL be selected by public
   auditable human sortition from an Enforcement-specific constitutionally
   qualified pool. Its competence review, pool administration, and sortition
   SHALL be materially independent of the Court and Verification Office.
7. A Verification or Enforcement candidate SHALL be a voluntary human
   constitutional member who publishes conflicts and material dependencies,
   satisfies a public human-administered competence standard, and for three
   years has held no HCA College, Court, opposite-office, succession-claimant,
   emergency-command, continuity-custodian, institutional
   artificial-intelligence-operation, or material institutional-contracting
   role.
8. A five-human ad hoc panel selected by public human sortition from
   noncandidate, nonofficeholding constitutional members SHALL verify compliance
   with each selection process. Neither permanent Office SHALL verify its own
   selection.
9. Removal from either Office SHALL require a five-human independent fact panel
   selected by public human sortition to find incapacity, serious misconduct,
   or constitutional disqualification by clear and convincing evidence.
10. Verification removal SHALL also require a twenty-one-human nonconflicted
    sortition jury, with seventeen members constituting quorum and approval by
    two thirds of the full jury. Enforcement removal SHALL instead require
    fixed constitutional membership confirmation, with participation exceeding
    one half and majority approval of valid votes.
11. The next eligible same-channel alternate SHALL fill the remainder of a
    vacant term. Exhaustion of alternates SHALL repeat only the ordinary
    selection process constitutionally authorized for that Office.
12. Any constitutional member MAY bring one expedited Court challenge to an
    Office selection, removal, replacement, selector composition, sortition,
    vacancy determination, or Verification or Enforcement act. Whole-Court
    conflict SHALL invoke the conflict tribunal in Section III.3.
13. Noncompliance MAY result in disclosure, expedited constitutional review,
   restoration, and bounded personal or institutional accountability.
14. The Court MAY invalidate an unlawful appointment, suspend unlawful authority,
   declare a vacancy, and order the separately authorized constitutional
   selection process to occur.
15. The Court SHALL NOT:
   a. appoint a replacement, unless a separate express constitutional rule
      grants a narrowly defined emergency selection function;
   b. create a constitutional office or foundational authority;
   c. redesign institutional ends;
   d. create or transfer amendment or refounding authority;
   e. exercise continuing managerial control; or
   f. impose open-ended diversification or restructuring.
16. Replacement SHALL ordinarily be performed by a separately authorized human
   selector. Any exceptional emergency selection function SHALL identify its
   human selector, trigger, eligibility, scope, challenge path, and nonrenewable
   expiry, and SHALL NOT enlarge Court jurisdiction or create precedent.
17. The Court MAY require disclosure, verification, recusal, independence
   safeguards, or narrowly bounded diversification only to remedy a demonstrated
   constitutional violation.
18. A diversification remedy SHALL be necessary, proportionate, specific,
   independently reviewable, and time-bounded where appropriate. It SHALL NOT
   appoint personnel, create or reallocate constitutional offices, determine
   institutional ends, control ordinary management, or alter amendment or
   refounding constituencies, thresholds, or authority.
19. Formal machine validity and nominal human approval SHALL NOT establish
    constitutional legitimacy or cure substantive machine domination.
20. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate case administration, evidence,
    hearings, compliance milestones, reporting, and verification methods. It
    SHALL NOT enlarge jurisdiction or remedies, transfer appointment power to a
    reviewer, or convert a remedy into institutional management.

## Article IV — Epistemic Integrity, Provenance, Dissent, and Effective Power

### Section IV.1 — Attributable Constitutional Records

1. Every material constitutional or institutional decision SHALL preserve:
   a. attributable human authorization;
   b. the evidence and reasons materially relied upon;
   c. viable alternatives considered;
   d. material dissent and unresolved uncertainty;
   e. the scope and source of claimed authority;
   f. the applicable time limit and review path;
   g. material machine assistance and dependency;
   h. precedent relied upon or created;
   i. supersession and correction history; and
   j. procedural regularity sufficient for human contestation and review.
2. Required records SHALL be durable, accessible, attributable, and
   comprehensible to the humans governed by the decision.
3. Correction or supersession SHALL preserve the prior record and SHALL NOT
   erase the reasons, dissent, authority claim, or effective-power conditions
   under which it arose.
4. Technical validation MAY establish conformity with a formal rule. It SHALL
   NOT establish constitutional legitimacy, human judgment, or lawful authority.

### Section IV.2 — Independence, Machine Domination, and Effective-Power Review

1. Actors and channels SHALL be materially independent only when they lack
   common control and material dependency in evidence, data, models,
   infrastructure, identity, communications, financing, agenda, enforcement,
   and incentives.
2. Every material dependency SHALL be disclosed.
3. No validator, model, credential, dashboard, issuer, evidence store,
   communication path, or operator SHALL be the sole gatekeeper of a
   constitutional exercise or remedy.
4. Substantive machine domination exists when responsible humans cannot
   independently:
   a. understand the material basis of a decision;
   b. reconstruct its reasoning;
   c. reject the machine recommendation;
   d. choose a viable nonmachine alternative; or
   e. control the timing and effect of the decision.
5. A human signature, vote, or approval SHALL NOT cure substantive machine
   domination.
6. An independent human effective-power audit SHALL occur annually and upon:
   a. a material dependency change;
   b. evidence of coalition coordination or capture;
   c. repeated emergency action;
   d. prolonged interregnum;
   e. adjudicative drift; or
   f. concentration of constitutional-change activity.
7. An audit SHALL distinguish formal allocation from effective control and
   SHALL preserve evidence, reasons, dissent, and machine dependencies.
8. Remedies for dependency, coalition capture, machine domination, or
   effective-power takeover SHALL remain within Article III.
9. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate disclosures, audits, publication,
   and technical evidence. It SHALL NOT redefine material independence,
   substantive machine domination, or constitutional legitimacy.

## Article V — Emergency Authority

### Section V.1 — Predelegated Emergency Authority

1. Explicit prior human authorization SHALL be the ordinary source of emergency
   authority.
2. An emergency delegation SHALL identify its human source, eligible holder,
   scope, trigger, duration, revocation, review, and restoration conditions.
3. Necessity SHALL NOT become an ordinary source of authority or cure a
   defective delegation.
4. No emergency delegation MAY authorize amendment, refounding, succession, or
   the creation or transfer of foundational sovereignty.

### Section V.2 — Necessity Fallback

1. A necessity fallback MAY be exercised ONLY IF clear and convincing
   contemporaneous evidence establishes all of the following:
   a. imminent irreversible harm;
   b. absence of adequate action under existing authority;
   c. genuine unreachability of every competent authority and valid delegate;
   d. necessity and proportionality; and
   e. selection of the least harmful and least authority-expanding adequate
      measure.
2. Only a natural person with direct situational competence, no material
   conflict, and no role in manufacturing unreachability MAY exercise the
   fallback.
3. Two materially independent eligible humans SHALL concur when delay permits.
   One eligible human MAY act alone ONLY IF obtaining concurrence would
   materially increase imminent irreversible harm, and SHALL record why.
4. The actor SHALL attempt contact with every registered competent authority and
   delegate through at least two independent channels, unless physically
   impossible.
5. Neither an artificial-intelligence recommendation nor machine-generated
   evidence SHALL make a person eligible, satisfy the burden without human
   judgment, or create fallback authority.

### Section V.3 — Scope, Absolute Prohibitions, and Expiry

1. Necessity fallback authority SHALL be limited to the minimum act required for
   stabilization.
2. Necessity fallback authority SHALL NOT:
   a. amend or refound;
   b. create or transfer sovereignty;
   c. create succession authority or select a successor;
   d. renew or extend itself;
   e. create precedent;
   f. destroy or conceal constitutional records;
   g. obstruct review or restoration; or
   h. act beyond minimum stabilization.
3. Fallback authority SHALL expire when a competent authority or valid delegate
   becomes reachable, the imminent threat ceases, or the minimum stabilizing act
   is complete, whichever occurs first, and in every case no later than
   twenty-four hours unless valid ordinary or predelegated authority
   independently authorizes further action.
4. Repeated use SHALL NOT renew fallback authority and SHALL trigger interregnum
   review.
5. Emergency authority SHALL NOT become succession authority, foundational
   authority, or evidence of constitutional legitimacy.

### Section V.4 — Emergency Record, Review, and Remedies

1. An emergency actor SHALL preserve contemporaneous records of trigger
   evidence, contact attempts, alternatives, reasons, acts, effects, material
   machine assistance, and restoration.
2. The actor SHALL file the emergency record immediately and no later than six
   hours after stabilization.
3. Human constitutional review SHALL begin within twenty-four hours after
   filing.
4. The emergency actor SHALL bear the burden of proving by clear and convincing
   evidence every trigger and limit, including unreachability, necessity,
   proportionality, least harm, scope, and expiry.
5. Concealment or destruction of evidence SHALL create a rebuttable adverse
   inference but SHALL NOT dictate the merits without human judgment.
6. One expedited appeal MAY be taken.
7. A successful outcome SHALL NOT prove legitimacy, and a harmful outcome alone
   SHALL NOT prove invalidity.
8. Irreversible effects SHALL remain reviewable. Article III remedies MAY
   include disclosure, restitution, compensation, institutional correction, and
   a nonprecedential finding.
9. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate contact systems, filing, evidence
   capture, notice, and restoration administration without changing the
   constitutional trigger, burden, limits, or deadlines.

## Article VI — Continuity, Succession, and Interregnum

### Section VI.1 — Claimant-Neutral Continuity

1. During incapacity, loss, vacancy, or disputed succession, only valid and
   current ordinary grants not personal to the lost authority MAY continue, and
   only within their existing scope.
2. Continuity SHALL create no new authority and SHALL remain neutral among
   succession claimants.
3. Foundational grants, necessity-fallback grants, expired grants, grants
   revocable upon incapacity, and grants that purport to create succession
   authority SHALL be suspended.
4. During interregnum, no continuity actor MAY amend, refound, terminate the
   constitutional order, create foundational sovereignty, extend temporary
   authority, or effect succession outside the process established by this
   Article.
5. Capability, necessity, effective control, machine recommendation, or
   operational indispensability SHALL NOT convert continuity into succession.

### Section VI.2 — Predesignation, Proof, and Succession Council

1. Through the same human election that selects each HCA College member, the
   fixed constitutional membership SHALL select three public ranked alternates
   for that seat.
2. A ranked alternate SHALL be a current, attributable, reviewable, and
   revocable successor designation. Membership MAY revoke or replace an
   alternate through the same election process before a succession trigger.
3. The College SHALL maintain and publish the designation record as a
   ministerial duty. It SHALL NOT choose, reorder, veto, or make an alternate
   effective.
4. Only death or permanent loss proved under this Article, attributable
   voluntary resignation, final removal under Section I.1, or a final temporary-
   incapacity determination under this Article MAY initiate recognition of an
   alternate or a succession event for a College seat. Regular term expiry and
   alternate exhaustion MAY create only the election vacancies expressly
   governed by Section I.1 and SHALL NOT initiate alternate recognition. No
   other condition MAY create a College vacancy or succession event.
5. Temporary incapacity SHALL require clear and convincing evidence and a final
   determination by the Succession Council or applicable fallback panel. The
   next eligible membership-predesignated alternate MAY be recognized only as
   acting member. The principal SHALL retain the seat, and verified return SHALL
   restore authority under Section VI.4. Acting service SHALL NOT become
   removal, a new term, or permanent succession and SHALL NOT exceed the
   interregnum limit. A determination SHALL become final only when the appeal
   period expires without appeal or final review affirms it.
6. Permanent incapacity SHALL proceed only through removal under Section I.1.
   Death, permanent loss, identity, return, and successor identity SHALL require
   proof beyond a reasonable doubt from at least three materially independent
   sources. Resignation SHALL be voluntary and attributable.
7. Final removal SHALL create a permanent vacancy and succession trigger only
   after constituent confirmation and review finality under Section I.1.
8. Upon a valid trigger, the Succession Council or applicable fallback panel
   MAY recognize only the next eligible nonrecused membership-predesignated
   alternate. Recognition SHALL confirm constitutional compliance and SHALL NOT
   select or create foundational authority.
9. The petitioner, fact panel, confirming electorate, Court, conflict tribunal,
   Verification Office, Enforcement Office, and artificial-intelligence systems
   SHALL NOT choose, reorder, appoint, or veto the successor.
10. Exhaustion of alternates SHALL create a vacancy and invoke the special
   membership election required by Section I.1. It SHALL create no interim,
   emergency, continuity, machine, or effective-control authority.
11. A common issuer, operator, data source, infrastructure, incentive, or
   artificial-intelligence dependency SHALL defeat source independence.
12. A seven-human Succession Council SHALL consist of:
   a. two members selected by the constitutional membership;
   b. two members selected by ordinary human governance offices;
   c. two members selected by the Constitutional Court, no current adjudicator
      serving; and
   d. one member selected by human sortition from the qualified pool.
13. Five affirmative votes SHALL be required to determine a succession trigger
   or recognize a lawful predesignation. Six affirmative votes SHALL be
   required to conclude that succession is impossible and refer the matter to
   the explicit refounding process.
14. A claimant, emergency actor, beneficiary with a particularized conflict,
   evidence operator, or materially dependent person SHALL recuse.
15. The Council SHALL publish evidence, reasons, dissent, uncertainty,
   dependencies, and effective-power conditions. Evidence validation and
   succession judgment SHALL remain separate.
16. One appeal MAY be taken to the Constitutional Court.

### Section VI.3 — Non-Quorum Human Fallback

1. Before a succession trigger, thirty eligible constitutional members SHALL be
   placed in a public ranked fallback pool by auditable human sortition.
2. Pool eligibility SHALL use the Succession Council's human independence and
   conflict rules. Council members, continuity custodians, known successor
   claimants, emergency actors, and operators of materially dependent evidence
   or artificial-intelligence systems SHALL be excluded.
3. If the Council cannot achieve quorum after its ordinary alternates and
   recusals are exhausted, the first fifteen eligible and nonrecused persons in
   rank order SHALL form a fallback panel. Vacancies and recusals SHALL be
   filled in rank order.
4. Twelve panel members SHALL constitute quorum. Ten affirmative votes SHALL be
   required to determine a succession trigger or recognize only the next
   eligible, nonrecused, membership-predesignated alternate under Section
   VI.2. Exhaustion SHALL proceed only through the membership election required
   by Section VI.2 and SHALL create no successor-selection power.
5. The panel SHALL apply the evidence and proof burdens in Section VI.2, publish
   attributable reasons and dissent, and remain subject to one expedited Court
   appeal.
6. A sortition administrator or machine system SHALL have no discretion to
   alter the rank or select the successor.
7. If neither the Council nor the panel has made a constitutionally valid
   determination by day one hundred fifty of the interregnum, the panel SHALL
   record failure and issue public notice opening access to the explicit
   refounding process. Notice SHALL NOT authorize refounding.
8. Failure, vacancy, quorum loss, emergency, necessity, recommendation, or
   effective control SHALL create no foundational sovereignty or additional
   continuity authority.

### Section VI.4 — Interregnum Limit, Return, and Invalid Succession

1. Interregnum SHALL receive constitutional review at ninety days.
2. Continuity authority SHALL end no later than one hundred eighty days after
   interregnum begins.
3. Every surviving grant SHALL expire at its original expiry or at the
   one-hundred-eighty-day limit, whichever occurs first.
4. Reaching the limit SHALL require a lawful succession determination or access
   to explicit refounding and SHALL NOT transfer sovereignty automatically.
5. Failure to complete succession or refounding before the limit SHALL create no
   continuing, residual, emergency, or sovereign authority.
6. Final verification of return SHALL restore lawful authority immediately
   without consent of a continuity custodian. The Court MAY issue only a
   narrowly tailored stay necessary to prevent conflicting irreversible acts.
7. An invalid succession SHALL end prospective authority immediately.
8. Completed acts under an invalid succession SHALL receive act-specific review
   for good-faith reliance, beneficiary protection, reversibility, and
   constitutional harm.
9. Remedies MAY include invalidation, restoration, restitution, disclosure,
   replacement of an executor through a separately authorized human process,
   and reliance-protective prospective treatment. No remedy SHALL validate the
   succession.
10. Subordinate governance law MAY administer designation records, evidence,
    pools, scheduling, recounts, clocks, and notices without changing the human
    selectors, burdens, vote rules, or time limits.

## Article VII — Amendment and Constitutional Change

### Section VII.1 — Categories and Non-Waiver

1. Constitutional change SHALL occur only as:
   a. ordinary amendment, changing governance while preserving constitutional
      structure and foundational institutional identity;
   b. structural amendment, materially changing constitutional architecture
      while preserving foundational institutional identity; or
   c. explicit refounding, changing foundational institutional identity and
      acknowledging constitutional discontinuity.
2. A change to a refounding-level principle SHALL NOT be enacted as ordinary or
   structural amendment.
3. Artificial intelligence MAY assist analysis, drafting, simulation,
   provenance, and formal validation, but SHALL NOT authorize amendment or
   refounding.
4. Human approval SHALL NOT cure substantive machine domination of a
   constitutional-change process.
5. The Court MAY classify a proposed change and review compliance. It SHALL NOT
   amend or refound by interpretation.
6. Emergency authority SHALL NOT amend, refound, lower a constitutional-change
   threshold, or create continuity for an identity-changing act.
7. Succession SHALL NOT confer amendment or refounding authority.
8. No adjudicative, emergency, succession, implementation, or validation process
   MAY waive or substitute for the applicable change process.
9. Structural amendment SHALL be required to change HCA College composition,
   numbered seats, terms, nonrenewal, candidate eligibility other than the
   human-membership rule protected by paragraph 10, election
   electorate, participation or result rules, alternate ranking, tie handling,
   certification independence, no-holdover rule, removal cause or proof,
   fact-finding, constituent confirmation, review, removal finality, succession
   triggers, selector independence, separation of removal from replacement, or
   foundational-act membership-confirmation rules; Verification or Enforcement
   composition, selectors, terms, removal, replacement, or challenge paths; or
   a Schedule O entry, qualification, vote, or anti-manipulation protection.
10. Explicit refounding SHALL be required to change human sovereignty over
    foundational institutional ends, constitutional membership as the human
    source of HCA legitimacy, the human-only character of HCA College
    authority, or artificial-intelligence ineligibility to hold, exercise, or
    independently create foundational authority, including foundational
    sovereignty.
11. Ordinary law, ordinary amendment, adjudication, emergency, succession,
    continuity, implementation, precedent, vacancy, acquiescence, capability,
    reliance, or effective control SHALL NOT waive or alter the allocations in
    paragraphs 9 and 10.

### Section VII.2 — Amendment and Refounding Procedures

1. The amendment constituency SHALL be the constitutional membership fixed
   before proposal initiation. The refounding constituency SHALL be the same
   membership acting without delegation.
2. Ordinary amendment SHALL require:
   a. notice of at least fourteen days;
   b. participation exceeding one half of constitutional members; and
   c. approval by at least two thirds of valid votes.
3. Structural amendment SHALL require:
   a. participation of at least two thirds of constitutional members;
   b. approval by at least three quarters of valid votes;
   c. two approving votes at least thirty days apart; and
   d. at each vote, majority approval within each of three voting cohorts
      assigned by publicly auditable human sortition before deliberation and
      administered by materially independent authorities.
4. The three structural-amendment cohorts SHALL first be formed from the
   largest equal allocation of eligible constitutional members possible. If
   membership is not divisible by three, each remaining eligible human member
   SHALL be assigned among the cohorts solely by auditable random sortition.
   No human or artificial-intelligence actor MAY choose a remainder assignment
   discretionarily.
5. Remainder sortition MAY resolve remainder allocation only. It SHALL NOT
   alter cohort powers, thresholds, eligibility, constitutional authority, or
   amendment or refounding boundaries. Its attributable provenance SHALL be
   preserved and independently verifiable. Subordinate governance law MAY
   regulate the mechanism without changing these constitutional limits.
6. Refounding SHALL require:
   a. participation of at least three quarters of constitutional members;
   b. approval by at least four fifths of valid votes;
   c. two approving votes at least ninety days apart;
   d. a ballot expressly acknowledging constitutional discontinuity; and
   e. separate confirmation by affected human beneficiaries.
7. An affected human beneficiary SHALL be a natural person outside the fixed
   membership whose existing legal rights, obligations, access to institutional
   assets, or eligibility for material institutional benefits would be directly
   altered by refounding.
8. Before the first vote, the Verification Office SHALL determine and publish
   the affected-beneficiary roll from public criteria and evidence. Any member
   or claimed affected person MAY bring one expedited Court appeal. Voting SHALL
   NOT begin until timely challenges are final.
9. Affected-beneficiary confirmation SHALL require participation exceeding one
   half of the final roll and approval by more than one half of valid votes.
   Abstentions SHALL count only toward participation. Invalid ballots SHALL
   count toward neither denominator. A tie SHALL fail.
10. Every proposal SHALL have an attributable human sponsor and SHALL publish:
   a. its full text;
   b. a comprehensible statement of effects;
   c. material alternatives;
   d. material dissent;
   e. material machine assistance and dependencies; and
   f. the Court's classification before the first vote.
11. Deliberation SHALL be meaningful, public, and comprehensible. Every
   constitutional member SHALL have standing to challenge classification or
   procedure. One expedited appeal SHALL be final.
12. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate proposal filing, deliberation,
    voting, recounts, and publication without changing the constituencies,
    thresholds, waiting periods, concurrence, challenge, or non-waiver rules.

### Section VII.3 — Cumulative Effect and Semantic Drift

1. Amendment laundering is prohibited. Separately valid acts SHALL be reviewed
   together when their cumulative effect may cross a structural-amendment or
   refounding boundary.
2. Cumulative review SHALL include amendments, interpretations, implementation
   acts, delegation changes, and material effective-power shifts occurring
   within twenty-four months.
3. Older acts SHALL also be included when linked by express purpose, legal
   dependency, common sponsorship, financing, infrastructure, or necessary
   operational effect.
4. A time, lineage, dependency, or combined-effects trigger SHALL require Court
   review before further effect.
5. Higher-tier treatment SHALL be required when the combined effect materially
   changes office allocation, checks and balances, amendment conditions,
   protected human agency, beneficiary status, sovereignty, constitutional
   constraint, or another refounding-level principle.
6. A proponent seeking lower-tier treatment SHALL prove its adequacy by clear
   and convincing evidence.
7. The Court MAY suspend, remand, consolidate and reclassify, require renewed
   tier-compliant approval, restore lawful state, require disclosure, or grant
   reliance-protective prospective relief. It SHALL NOT authorize the change.
8. A human-led public semantic-drift audit SHALL occur every two years and SHALL
   compare constitutional text, precedent, practice, reliance, and effective
   power.
9. Material semantic drift SHALL trigger review and SHALL NOT itself amend the
   Constitution.
10. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate lineage records, comparison
    methods, filing, and publication without narrowing aggregation or changing
    classification authority.

## Article VIII — Refounding, Termination, Continuity of Obligations, and Constitutional Memory

### Section VIII.1 — Refounding-Level Principles

1. The following principles define foundational institutional identity and MAY
   be altered only through explicit human refounding:
   a. foundational institutional purpose and ends;
   b. ultimate human beneficiary status;
   c. human sovereignty over foundational ends;
   d. constitutional constraint of human and artificial power;
   e. the prohibition on capability, reliance, or effective control creating
      authority;
   f. the prohibition on artificial intelligence acquiring foundational
      sovereignty through succession, delegation, emergency, adjudication, or
      accretion;
   g. protected human agency;
   h. the distinction between adjudication and amendment;
   i. the distinction between amendment and refounding; and
   j. durable provenance and non-erasure of constitutional history.
2. These principles are not metaphysically immutable. Humans MAY alter them only
   by completing explicit refounding and acknowledging constitutional
   discontinuity.
3. Ordinary or structural amendment SHALL NOT alter, evade, suspend, or
   cumulatively defeat a refounding-level principle.
4. For purposes of paragraph 1.c and paragraph 1.f, constitutional membership
   as the constituent human root of HCA legitimacy, the human-only character of
   HCA College authority, and artificial-intelligence ineligibility to hold,
   exercise, or independently create foundational authority are
   refounding-level protections.

### Section VIII.2 — Termination and Transition Authority

1. A valid refounding act SHALL terminate the prior constitutional identity only
   at its declared effective time, which SHALL follow final certification of the
   result and final disposition of every timely challenge.
2. Before the second refounding vote, each of the three materially independent
   Court-selection channels SHALL publicly select two Transition Authority
   members and two ranked alternates. The constitutional membership SHALL select
   one member and two ranked alternates.
3. Current judges, refounding sponsors, and prior continuity custodians SHALL be
   ineligible. The final ballot SHALL identify the full slate and alternates.
4. Refounding SHALL NOT take effect unless the Verification Office certifies all
   seven members and at least seven alternates before voting closes. A challenge
   to certification MAY receive one expedited Court appeal before result
   certification.
5. The refounding act SHALL conditionally create the Transition Authority's
   mandate. The mandate SHALL activate prospectively at the effective time when
   the old identity terminates.
6. Five nonrecused members SHALL constitute quorum, and five affirmative votes
   SHALL authorize action.
7. Recusal SHALL create a vacancy filled immediately from the relevant
   channel's ranked alternates.
8. With fewer than five members, all action SHALL be suspended except record and
   asset preservation approved unanimously by at least three available,
   nonrecused members. With fewer than three members, no action SHALL be
   authorized.
9. The Transition Authority SHALL expire after one hundred eighty days, SHALL be
   strictly nonrenewable, and SHALL NOT participate in any process creating a
   later transition body.

### Section VIII.3 — Transition Accountability, Obligations, and Memory

1. The Transition Authority MAY preserve records, safety, assets, ordinary
   administration, and claims only as expressly authorized by the refounding
   act.
2. The Transition Authority SHALL NOT:
   a. amend or refound;
   b. alter or select foundational institutional ends;
   c. create or transfer sovereignty;
   d. irreversibly dispose of assets beyond ordinary preservation;
   e. veto new constitutional authority;
   f. self-renew; or
   g. create a permanent office unless the refounding act expressly authorizes
      that office.
3. The refounding act SHALL identify legitimate human institutions responsible
   for independent review and verification of the Transition Authority,
   enforcement of controlling decisions, and removal and replacement of
   transition members.
4. The institutions and challenge path required by paragraph 3 SHALL be
   constituted and capable of acting when refounding takes effect. The
   Transition Authority SHALL NOT appoint or control them. A refounding design
   without this accountability chain SHALL be constitutionally incomplete and
   SHALL NOT take effect.
5. Expiration SHALL NOT enlarge continuity authority. If the new order is not
   fully operative at expiration, only authority independently and expressly
   granted by the refounding act MAY survive.
6. Lawful obligations of the prior order SHALL presumptively survive unless they
   are impossible, dependent on the terminated identity, or expressly
   reconsidered by the new order with reasons and affected-party review.
7. Institutional assets SHALL remain in custodial trust for human beneficiaries
   pending lawful allocation.
8. Good-faith reliance MAY receive prospective protection only when consistent
   with protected human agency and beneficiary interests. Bad-faith or
   capture-based reliance SHALL receive no protection.
9. Remedies MAY include restoration, restitution, compensation, continuation of
   claims, invalidation, disclosure, and removal of a transition custodian.
10. Refounding SHALL permanently preserve and attribute the prior constitutional
    text, authorizations, decisions, reasons, material dissent, claims,
    supersession history, formal and effective-power record, and transition
    acts.
11. Refounding SHALL expressly acknowledge discontinuity and SHALL NOT erase,
    falsify, or obscure the prior constitutional order or its history.
12. Subordinate governance law MAY regulate transition reporting, claims,
    assets, records, notice, and replacement administration. It SHALL NOT create
    or extend transition authority, alter foundational ends, weaken independent
    accountability, or erase constitutional history.
