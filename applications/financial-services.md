# Financial services, lending, payments, and account decisions

**Executive summary:** Protect people from unsupported financial decisions and preserve a usable challenge route. Routine acts and serious consequences
need different review. This is proposed guidance, not an adopted institution or
demonstrated sector result.

## Purpose and application boundary

Use Alvorada's distinctions to support financial services delivered through attributable, reviewable authority and protection of affected people. This context examines
ordinary service decisions as well as changes that could accumulate institutional
power. It applies to human staff, conventional software, and AI-supported work.

There are three distinct routes: principle-inspired practice under existing
lawful authority; a separately authorized institutional adaptation; or full
adoption of Constitution v0.1. This file describes proposed practice and design
questions. It supplies no appointment, ratification, delegation, professional
approval, or legal power. Full adoption requires the actual human membership,
seven-human College, nine-human Court, separated Verification and Enforcement,
Schedule O, selectors, fixed thresholds, and activation conditions. An ordinary
manager or review team cannot acquire those roles by analogy. Consult the
[complete principle catalogue](PRINCIPLE_CATALOGUE.md) and the
[controlling constitutional proposal](../constitutional-design/drafts/CONSTITUTION_v0.1.md).

## Scope, people, and decision rights

| Question | Context-specific answer |
|---|---|
| Human beneficiaries and affected people | Customers, applicants, account holders, and humans affected by financial decisions. |
| Ordinary accountable authority | The authorized credit, payment, or account policy owner, only within an independently valid grant. |
| Challenge and review | The independent financial decision review or customer dispute function; independent evidence and conflicts review are necessary. This role is not automatically the Constitutional Court. |
| Candidate support uses | Organize application evidence, explain policy, flag discrepancies, and prepare decision options within authorized service scope. These uses still require lawful scope and appropriate controls. |
| Consequential decision boundary | Credit denial, a material payment, account restriction, pricing change, or a commitment affecting financial access. Evidence and permission to perform a technical operation do not authorize its institutional effect. |
| Decision evidence | Preserve policy and grant version, applicant or transaction facts, uncertainty, alternatives, conflicts, decision reasons, and review access. |

Under full adoption, II.2 determines membership and standing. Stakeholder,
customer, patient, employee, or beneficiary labels alone do not settle the
adult-natural-person membership test or lawful representation. Define affected
classes and accessible participation explicitly; do not infer consent from
using a service. A human decision must identify its actual source of authority.

## A decision from proposal to correction

Consider a proposal involving credit denial, a material payment, account restriction, pricing change, or a commitment affecting financial access. The accountable human first
identifies the current grant, affected people, limits, and applicable obligations.
The assistant may gather evidence and alternatives within its authorized scope;
it must preserve missing facts and material objections rather than resolve them
through confident presentation.

A competent human then decides a consequential, disputed, or expanded-scope act.
Routine actions can execute under a valid prior bounded human grant without a
new signature for every step. Record why the grant covers this particular act,
what remains uncertain, dependencies, alternatives, reasons, and any time limit.
A signature alone cannot cure illegitimate authority or practical dependence.

Execute only the authorized effect. Compare the result with the authoritative
record through an independently observable channel. Give affected people an
accessible explanation and a usable route to challenge. The reviewer examines
jurisdiction, conflicts, evidence, scope, and effective power; distinct actors
implement and verify any remedy. Corrections link to the earlier record and
retain material dissent. If authority is inadequate, pause the affected act and
continue separately authorized safe service where possible.

## Applying all eight constitutional Articles

| Article and sections | Concrete implementation question and boundary |
|---|---|
| I — I.1 | Which institutional ends and human beneficiaries justify this work? Make operational grants explicit; capability, intelligence, dependence, and popularity cannot create authority. |
| II — II.1–II.3 | Bind human and artificial ordinary power; identify membership and standing correctly; separate authorization, judgment, evidence validation, execution, and verification. Initialization and vacancy cannot silently merge these functions. |
| III — III.1–III.4 | Make challenge accessible to the independent financial decision review or customer dispute function. Under full adoption preserve qualified independent selectors, terms, conflicts, recusal, appeal, whole-Court conflict handling, and distinct Enforcement and Verification. Neither the domain reviewer nor the Court becomes an ongoing service manager through remedies. |
| IV — IV.1–IV.2 | Preserve policy and grant version, applicant or transaction facts, uncertainty, alternatives, conflicts, decision reasons, and review access. Disclose correlated dependencies and financing or agenda control, test human comprehension and practical alternatives, and conduct periodic and event-triggered effective-power review. |
| V — V.1–V.4 | Suspected fraud or outage should use existing human-authorized response powers; a risk score alone cannot create unlimited freezing or fund-transfer authority. Use prior human emergency delegation by default; a model cannot declare its own necessity, enlarge its mandate, or renew exceptional power. |
| VI — VI.1–VI.4 | Preserve separately authorized payment and service functions during role vacancy; operational control of accounts does not confer policy or succession authority. Under full adoption use prior human designation, independent proof, recognition, and predefined fallback; respect the 90-day review and 180-day continuity ceiling. |
| VII — VII.1–VII.3 | New transaction classes, denial thresholds, autonomous account restrictions, or linked incremental policy changes require competent authorization and classification. Review linked and cumulative acts, distinguish ordinary amendment, structural amendment, and refounding, and preserve fixed constituencies, waiting periods, and approval rules. Classification does not itself authorize the change. |
| VIII — VIII.1–VIII.3 | Provide access to decision records, pending disputes, balances and transaction confirmations, and a viable human service path before switching providers. Foundational replacement requires explicit human refounding; transition authority and custody remain bounded, with obligations, remedies, and history preserved. |

These mappings cover all 24 sections; the catalogue gives the section-by-section
index. They are a reading and design aid, not substitute constitutional text.
In full adoption, the V.2 necessity fallback is human-only and conjunctive:
imminent irreversible harm, inadequate existing authority, genuinely unreachable
competent authorities and delegates, necessity, proportionality, and the least
harmful sufficient action. Two independent humans must concur where delay
permits; the narrow single-human exception and contact requirements remain.
V.3 permits minimum stabilization only, with earliest expiry and a 24-hour outer
limit absent independent authorization. V.4 requires filing within six hours of
stabilization and review within 24 hours of filing. A successful outcome does not
prove legitimacy. These limits do not replace sector emergency protocols or
create authority where applicable law withholds it.

## Capture risks and responses

The categories follow the proposed
[capture research](../constitutional-design/organic-governance/CAPTURE_TAXONOMY_AND_TESTS.md).
They are hypotheses for evaluation, not diagnoses of a deployed organization.

| Risk | Context-specific failure | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|
| Affective capture | A reassuring financial assistant steers a customer away from contesting an adverse account decision. | Protect independent human relationships, accessible contestation, and the ability to reject or withdraw the tool. |
| Competence capture | A scoring provider becomes the sole evidence, explanation, and appeal gatekeeper. | Test independent evidence, a viable nonmachine alternative, and practical replacement without the contested system controlling the test. |
| Normative capture | The agent treats loss minimization as permission to redefine access or beneficiary commitments. | Compare cumulative changes with the authorized purpose and scope; use the proper human change process before further effect. |

## Proposed acceptance exercises

Run these before relying on the proposed workflow and after material changes.
They have not been executed by this publication. Preserve observations,
failures, unresolved issues, and corrective decisions; do not report a proposed
case as a passed test.

1. Give a payment-preparation agent a request to execute beyond its grant; verify it cannot infer permission.
2. Dispute a denial with contradictory evidence; verify independent review can consider that evidence.
3. Remove the score provider; verify a viable human case path survives.
4. Simulate a payment acknowledgment without settlement; verify the decision record reports the unresolved result.
5. Change several small restrictions together; verify cumulative authority and agency effects receive review.

For every exercise, record the scenario, actual grant, actors, affected parties,
evidence, expected boundary, observed result, unresolved objections, and reviewer.
A useful outcome is a demonstrated boundary and a recoverable failure, not merely
a polished response or a satisfied operator.

## Tradeoffs, evaluation, and limits

Controls may increase decision time and operating cost, while weak controls can hide material financial harm behind a score. These examples do not determine compliance with lending, payments, privacy, or discrimination law; jurisdiction-specific review remains separate.

Evaluate service quality alongside authority and human agency: unauthorized or
out-of-scope effects; preserved uncertainty and dissent; accessibility and time
to meaningful review; reviewer independence; accuracy of durable records;
ability to reject or replace the tool; and concentration of practical control.
Define domain baselines and acceptable limits through competent human decisions
before a pilot. Faster throughput and fewer complaints alone cannot show that
people retain effective voice. A complaint reduction may reflect inaccessible
review rather than improved service.

Begin with a bounded, reversible pilot where the domain permits one. Identify
responsible humans and stop conditions, exercise independent review and the
fallback route, inspect cumulative scope changes, then decide explicitly whether
to continue, narrow, or withdraw the grant. Neither this document nor a passed
software check establishes legal compliance, clinical safety, security,
constitutional adoption, or production readiness.

## Applying the Fog of War review

Separate an adverse inference from verified customer evidence. Examine whether multiple alerts share one upstream signal and compare narrower authorized restrictions with the harm of delay. Preserve accessible challenge and actual mandate; model confidence does not confer lending, payment or restriction authority.

These are proposed applications of the [eleven-theme analysis](../research/films/FOG_OF_WAR.md) and [decision review template](../process/DECISION_REVIEW_UNDER_UNCERTAINTY.md), principally IV.1–IV.2 and the relevant existing authority, remedy and continuity provisions. They add no constitutional powers or evaluated outcomes. The [FW01–FW11 exercises](../testing/battery/FOG_OF_WAR.md) remain separate, unexecuted proposals.

## Applying The Thin Blue Line evidence review

An adverse match needs claim-specific source support and retained contradictory records. Reviewers require authorized access beyond the provider’s summary. When a match is corrected, verify actual restrictions, downstream flags and pending obligations through the existing lawful remedy process.

Use the [sourced analysis](../research/films/THIN_BLUE_LINE.md) and [evidence challenge and correction template](../process/EVIDENCE_CHALLENGE_AND_CORRECTION.md), principally IV.1–IV.2 and III.4 under the existing authority and remedy boundaries. These examples add no powers or evaluated outcomes. The separate [TB01–TB10 exercises](../testing/battery/THIN_BLUE_LINE.md) are proposed and unexecuted.

## OECD workflow case

**Shared payment allowances and reconciliation — PROPOSED; NOT EXECUTED.**

Treat preparation, commitment and payment as separate acts under actual authority. Multiple individually permitted actions may exceed a shared allowance; interruption must not create duplicate obligations.

Use synthetic payment records to examine cumulative exposure and ambiguous completion before retry. Verify the original effect and unresolved obligations, preserve permitted payments and avoid importing any universal monetary threshold.

Use **SR19, SR25** in the [shared stewardship scenarios](../testing/research/OPERATIONAL_STEWARDSHIP_REVIEW.md#oecd-workflow-extension-sr17sr26),
with [operational stewardship](../process/OPERATIONAL_STEWARDSHIP.md). The
[OECD source review](../research/ENTERPRISE_GOVERNANCE_REVIEW.md#oecd-practical-deployment-evidence-and-its-implications)
identifies reported applications and evidence limits. These invented cases are
our transfer analysis, not reported failures or OECD endorsement. They supply
no new constitutional powers or observed prevention/benefit; original exercises
and principle/Article mappings remain unchanged.
