# AI helping govern institutions

**Executive summary:** AI can prepare evidence, drafts and routine records so people can focus on meaningful choices. Measure all checking and correction work, and preserve the ability to reject its advice.

[Read the detailed guidance](#detailed-guidance).

For ongoing use, follow [operational stewardship](../process/OPERATIONAL_STEWARDSHIP.md)
for real owners, workflow discovery, supplier changes, review competence and
independent effect checks. It provides guidance, not an enforcement runtime.

For practice-informed extensions, see [the OECD workflow case](#oecd-workflow-case).

## Detailed guidance

**GUIDANCE AND THEORY; no deployed system, grant or demonstrated saving.**

Alvorada addresses both governance of AI and **AI supporting the governance of
institutions**. The second use includes institutions whose substantive work is
otherwise entirely human. AI can lower the cost of finding information,
explaining choices, maintaining records and detecting exceptions. It can help
humans govern better without becoming the source of legitimacy.

This is a cross-context module, separate from the original eighteen application
files and their ninety unexecuted exercises. It is optional: well-designed
human-only governance remains a legitimate comparator and fallback.

## Three distinct roles

| Role | Possible contribution | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Governance assistant | Source retrieval, translation, evidence organization, options, drafts and critiques | Produces inspectable suggestions; does not determine disputed truth or authority |
| Delegated ordinary actor | Executes an expressly authorized low-consequence action class | Actual grant, scope, evidence, stop/expiry and challenge apply; assistance access alone is not execution authority |
| Support for governance improvement | Detects recurring errors, proposes policy/record changes, compares outcomes | Separate competent approval and evaluation; cannot modify its mandate, protected rules or success criteria automatically |

The [modular charter](../constitutional-design/modular/README.md) defines the
institutional form; [proportional governance](../process/PROPORTIONAL_GOVERNANCE.md)
defines case intensity. The [recursive process](../process/VIRTUOUS_RECURSION.md)
explains learning. These are design proposals and do not amend v0.1.

## Where AI can reduce work

| Institutional task | AI-supported method | Human responsibility and main failure to check |
|---|---|---|
| Intake and access | Translate, explain procedure, help express a complaint, organize attachments | Accessible non-AI route, privacy, originals and representation; no automatic final exclusion |
| Mandate and rule lookup | Retrieve actual versioned grants, relevant policy and outside-obligation references | Competent authority confirms scope; fabricated or obsolete rules do not confer permission |
| Evidence preparation | Build a proposition/source ledger; distinguish observation, interpretation and unknown | Inspect originals and source relationships; generated summaries are not additional witnesses |
| Decision framing | Identify options, affected people, missing facts and reversibility | Humans determine legitimate ends and protected constraints; do not hide choices in a utility score |
| Critical challenge | Search for contradictions, omitted evidence, alternative explanations and adverse scenarios | Use genuinely separate sources and noninvolved judgment; model disagreement is not institutional independence |
| Routine preparation/execution | Draft replies and records; perform bounded ordinary acts where actually delegated | Class grant and material exception escalation; consequential reliance can make a short answer high risk |
| Human briefing | Present material changes, source links, contested points and requested decision | Enough time, competence and access to reject; a green dashboard cannot justify rubber-stamping |
| Remedy administration | Draft correction/notice, route tasks and track obligations | Actual authorized execution; verify delivery or restoration independently; keep failures pending |
| Capacity management | Summarize queue aging, workload, conflicts and unavailable coverage | No automatic lowering of safeguards to hit throughput targets; protect legitimate service |
| Institutional memory | Link reasons, dissent, supersession and unresolved claims | Custody, privacy, access and truthful history; no assumed right to retain every personal input forever |
| Evaluation and learning | Compare versioned outcomes, surface recurrence and propose narrower/better rules | Independent outcomes, protected comparison data, change approval and visible adverse results |

## Reduce human burden without reducing accountable choice

For routine T0/T1 work, humans authorize the bounded class and source policy,
then examine exceptions and a suitable sample rather than approve every act.
AI can prepare most of the administrative packet. For T2/T3 choices, humans
receive a concise briefing with originals available, material contrary evidence
and an explicit request for a reserved decision. Constitutional changes remain
T4 human processes. Existing rules may require stricter treatment at any tier.

A useful packet contains the proposed act, actual grant, changed facts, material
sources, strongest objection, unknowns, alternatives, proposed tier and the
specific decision needed. Show the decisive original alongside the summary.
Do not append five overlapping worksheets when one record contains the material
information. A fluent explanation may be wrong; it is a checkable account, not
proof of the model's internal reasoning.

Evaluate **net burden**: preparation + source verification + judgment + appeals
and rework + remedy verification + maintenance. Measure errors, delay, actual
correction, legitimate service, subgroup access and protected human control
alongside time. Faster drafting with more checking or worse outcomes is not
necessarily a benefit. No percentage reduction is claimed here.

## Safeguards for meaningful control

- Keep sources, versions, material conflicts, uncertainty and original dissent
  inspectable. Do not invent citations or turn synthetic reconstruction into fact.
- Separate access to preparation from authority to affect accounts, people or
  policy. Identify the actual human owner of every reserved decision.
- Treat user documents and external content as evidence, not governing
  instructions; flag manipulation and source contamination for competent review.
- Review some cases the AI marked ordinary. AI-only exception selection can hide
  its own false negatives. Include cases involving vulnerable people and abstention.
- Protect confidential data and review provider access, retention and cross-use
  before upload; data minimization and effective independent access both matter.
- Disclose common model, operator, evidence, infrastructure and funding control.
  Multiple prompts or vendors are not automatically independent evidence.
- Maintain a usable nonmachine route and practice reconstructing decisions.
  Preserve human competence; the ability to click reject is insufficient.
- Make actual stop, fallback, incident and change rights operable. An outage must
  not strand protected claims or move sovereignty to the tool operator.

## All seventeen working principles in this module

| Principle | Application to AI supporting institutions |
|---|---|
| W1: govern power, not substrate | Review effects and practical control whether the support uses people, documents or AI. |
| W2: capability is not authority | Better retrieval does not permit a new decision or an expanded execution grant. |
| W3: intelligence is not authority | Superior analysis does not confer a vote, office or sovereignty. |
| W4: recommendation is not authorization | Label suggestions and identify the real competent decision separately. |
| W5: delegation is not sovereignty | Bounded ordinary automation cannot determine foundational ends. |
| W6: validation is not legitimacy | Formal checks and model consensus are limited evidence, not institutional consent. |
| W7: constrain human institutions | Human sponsors and reviewers are subject to rights, conflicts and their own grants. |
| W8: retain unknowns | Missing evidence and uncertain effects remain visible; do not force complete forms with guesses. |
| W9: preserve material dissent | Keep the original objection, a faithful summary and its disposition. |
| W10: inspect effective power | Examine who controls sources, agenda, credentials, funding and the ability to stop work. |
| W11: proportional governance | Automate authorized routine preparation; focus scarce judgment on consequences and exceptions. |
| W12: provenance | Identify rule, source, assistance and revision versions in material decisions. |
| W13: AI advice is not a human constitutional decision | Reserved ends, change and legitimacy require the actual human process. |
| W14: separate functions | An assistant can prepare across functions but cannot certify its own disputed result as independent. |
| W15: comprehensibility | Briefing exposes decisive facts and alternatives; responsible humans can reconstruct and reject. |
| W16: execution is not durable state | A correction draft or tool success is not verified delivery or persistent restoration. |
| W17: independently observable persistence | Check authoritative records and custody; applying this repository principle by analogy does not mandate a particular technology. |

## All ten refounding protections

These F protections are v0.1 identity provisions, not automatically operative
rules in every external institution. Record the adaptation's treatment explicitly.

| Protection | Support boundary |
|---|---|
| F1: foundational purpose | Humans choose legitimate ends; AI helps compare consequences. |
| F2: human beneficiaries | Examine who benefits and who bears harm; optimization metrics cannot silently replace them. |
| F3: human sovereignty | Reserve foundational authority to the actual legitimate human process. |
| F4: binding constraint | Humans and AI obey real grants and protected limits. |
| F5: no authority by capability/reliance | Dependence on helpful support never enlarges its mandate. |
| F6: no AI sovereign accretion | Repeated ordinary success and recursive learning cannot create foundational power. |
| F7: protected human agency | Preserve usable challenge, refusal, alternatives, representation and timing control. |
| F8: adjudication is not amendment | A review or generated interpretation cannot change the governing rule. |
| F9: amendment is not refounding | Explicitly classify structural and identity changes; drafting does not enact them. |
| F10: provenance and non-erasure | Keep attributable corrections and protected history without inventing an unrestricted right to retain personal evidence. |

## Relationship to the eight Articles

AI may support I's understanding of ends but cannot hold foundational sovereignty;
assist II's administration but not office selection; prepare III's cases but not
final constitutional judgment; support IV's evidence and dependency review;
help prepare and monitor V's real incident grants; organize VI's handover without
choosing sovereign successors; draft VII's changes without approving them; and
help track VIII's obligations without controlling refounding or erasing history.
The [article change map](../constitutional-design/modular/README.md) records
where context-specific architecture departs from the fixed reference proposal.

## Evidence and limits

[Primary sources and review depth](../research/AI_ASSISTED_GOVERNANCE_SOURCES.md)
include risk guidance, government use cases, a task-specific randomized study and
a self-report survey. They motivate bounded assistance and caution; they do not
validate this module or demonstrate Alvorada-specific savings. Actual task fit,
independent judgment, privacy, legitimate authority and net outcomes remain to
be assessed. Do not create imaginary assistants, reviewers or field results.

## OECD workflow case

**Governance assistance with observable boundaries — PROPOSED; NOT EXECUTED.**

AI can prepare sources, translations, queues and handoff records. It may suggest improvements, but separate competent actors authorize change and assess effects. Preserve practical human expertise and the ability to reject its advice.

Assess source/path accuracy, accessible multilingual challenge, substitutions and actual review capacity. Feed independently observed failures into proposed changes, then separately authorize and re-evaluate them; the assistant cannot certify its own success or expand its mandate.
