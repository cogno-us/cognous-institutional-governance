# Decision-making research

**Executive summary:** Make choices explicit, compare real alternatives and learn from outcomes. Business and econometrics research offers useful methods, with different evidence strengths and limits.

## Detailed analysis

**LATER SUPPORTING RESEARCH — PROPOSED PRACTICES; NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT**

Reviewed 2026-10-04 at the maintainer's direction. Alvorada is ultimately a tool
for institutional decision-making: framing a choice, comparing permissible
options, making an accountable decision, observing its effects and correcting
it when warranted. Its constitutional architecture supplies the authority and
accountability conditions around that work.

This selected review covers eleven works from Harvard Business Review, MIT
Sloan Management Review, Stanford decision analysis and management research,
entrepreneurship, organizational learning and econometrics. It is not a
systematic review or an assessment of every article from those institutions.
Publication venue is not an evidence grade or an endorsement of Alvorada.

The [follow-up critique response](GOVERNANCE_CRITIQUE.md) adds two scoped
readings on formal/effective authority and bounded initiative, and practical
guides for delay, adoption incentives and substantive record value. The
initial eleven-work register remains distinct from those later additions.

## Inclusion decisions and reasons

The following are **our recommendations**, informed by the linked studies, not
findings that those studies tested this framework.

| Candidate practice | Disposition | Reason and retained cost |
|---|---|---|
| Define the decision, affected people and actual decision rights | Include in supporting workflow | Connects analysis to accountable action; defining rights can reveal an unresolved grant. |
| State objectives and compare real alternatives before committing | Include | Makes a recommendation contestable; detailed comparison takes time. |
| Challenge material assumptions and source dependence | Include | Extends existing uncertainty review; checking every immaterial claim would waste capacity. |
| Preserve initial judgments and material disagreement | Include where proportionate | Makes later changes explainable; records alone do not remove hierarchy or fear. |
| State a testable expectation and what would change the choice | Include for empirical claims | Enables correction; not every objective or obligation is empirically testable. |
| Assess benefits, burdens and distribution separately | Include | Avoids hiding harm in an aggregate metric; legitimate tradeoffs still need human judgment. |
| Compare against competent conventional practice | Include in evaluation | Tests added value rather than an intentionally weak opponent; baseline collection costs effort. |
| Use numerical models or experiments | Conditional | Requires a suitable question, data, assumptions, competence and actual authority. |
| Automatic weighted score, mandatory experiment or universal pass threshold | Do not include | Would conceal normative choices or exceed the available evidence. |
| New constitutional rights, offices or powers | Defer to the competent human normative process | Research recommendations cannot amend the proposal. |

## What this adds

The practical contribution is a connection between **how to choose**, **who may
choose**, and **how the choice can be challenged and corrected**. Familiar
decision methods already exist. Alvorada's potential value is their integration
with traceable authority, effective independence and institutional continuity,
not invention of decision science. Whether that integration improves decisions
enough to justify its burden remains unmeasured.

These are later readings. They do not retroactively establish the origins of
the original principles, alter v0.1 or change the status of existing results.
External publications retain their own rights. Only original summaries,
analysis and templates are added; no article, proprietary checklist or licensed
teaching material is reproduced.
