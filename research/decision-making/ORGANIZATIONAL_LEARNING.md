# Organizational learning: evidence, experimentation and dissent

**QUALIFIED RESEARCH FINDINGS AND OUR PROPOSED APPLICATIONS**

See the [source register](SOURCES.md) for links and review depth.

## What the reviewed works establish

**DM04 — Pfeffer and Sutton, HBR/Stanford.** Their evidence-based management
argument challenges untested tradition and selective experience as grounds for
management advice. We take this as a reason to keep a claim's evidence and
applicability visible, not proof that more documentation causes better outcomes.

**DM05 — Bazerman and Luca, MIT SMR.** The publisher describes experimentation
as a way to assess offerings, policies and uncertain ideas. This supports a
candidate learning practice, conditional on a suitable design and actual permission.

**DM06 — Camuffo and colleagues.** The 2024 replication combines four randomized
trials involving 759 firms, including the original study. Entrepreneurs were
taught to formulate theories, derive hypotheses and test them. The study reports
increased idea termination and a nuanced pattern of radical pivots. These are
specific outcomes of a training intervention; more stopping or pivoting is not
automatically better governance, and the trials do not test Alvorada.

**DM07 — Edmondson, Harvard.** The 1999 field study associates team psychological
safety with learning behavior in 51 manufacturing teams. The paper explicitly
limits causal inference because its survey design is cross-sectional, and warns
about generalizing from one company. It motivates investigating usable dissent,
not a causal guarantee from a statement that employees may speak up.

## Our inclusion recommendation

For a consequential empirical claim, state a prediction before action and record
the observation that would lead the competent human to reconsider. Preserve
negative observations and termination reasons. Distinguish an idea being stopped
because its premise failed from withdrawal caused by inaccessible review,
resource exhaustion or retaliation.

A dissent log needs a usable route to the person who can act, adequate evidence
access and an observed response. Examine whether participants can disagree with
superiors in practice. Collect adverse and missing accounts as well as successful
challenges. Protect sensitive reports through the actual authorized process;
do not make public attribution a prerequisite for raising a concern.

Experiments require a bounded empirical question, appropriate participants,
clear comparators and stop conditions. Testing a service message in an isolated
simulation is different from changing people's benefits or exposing them to
harm. This repository supplies neither sector authority nor participant consent.

## Fit, tradeoffs and evidence needed

IV.1 provides a place for unknowns, objections and correction history; IV.2
directs attention to evidence and incentive dependence; III concerns competent
review and verified remedies. The existing [cross-disciplinary review](../../process/CROSS_DISCIPLINARY_REVIEW.md)
adds participation and capacity questions. Cultural trust and resources remain
operational conditions that a constitutional paragraph alone cannot deliver.

Hypothesis discipline can become rigid if important questions are unmeasurable
or the wrong objective is fixed too early. Encouraging dissent without capacity
to investigate can produce burden without correction. Assess actual access,
review time, legitimate service and delivered remedies separately using the
[prepared institutional program](../../testing/programs/INSTITUTIONAL_ASSESSMENT.md).
