# Decision process: objectives, alternatives and judgment

**SOURCE INTERPRETATION AND PROPOSED PRACTICE — NOT VALIDATED EFFECTIVENESS**

Source IDs resolve to the [reviewed bibliography and access limits](SOURCES.md).

## What the reviewed works contribute

**DM01 — Stanford decision analysis.** Howard distinguishes normative accounts
of good choice from descriptions of how people actually choose. His abstract
discusses formulating problems, eliciting preferences, generating alternatives
and representing dependencies. This supports studying a clear decision
structure; it supplies no measured benefit for Alvorada.

**DM02 — HBR proposal review.** Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony propose reviewing
the reasoning process behind a team's recommendation, including interests,
contrary evidence and alternatives. We use the general problem, not their
copyrighted checklist or the publisher's advertised return improvement.

**DM03 — MIT SMR strategic judgment.** The publisher describes a disciplined
sequence for complex evaluative judgments. We recommend explicit ordering in
our own workflow; the full article's detailed procedure has not been assessed.

## Our interpretation for Alvorada

A decision record should answer three different questions: was the choice
within actual authority, were the reasons supportable with information available
then, and what happened afterward? None substitutes for the others. A fortunate
outcome can accompany weak reasoning; sound reasoning can encounter a bad
realization. An accurately predicted benefit cannot create a missing grant.

Before analyzing an option, define the actual choice and authorized objectives.
Distinguish objectives from constraints and outcome measures. The competent
human must resolve disputed values; an analyst should expose them rather than
bury them in weights. Membership and standing are determined by the relevant
institution, not a convenient list of workshop attendees.

Compare genuinely available options, including narrower or reversible action
and deferral where feasible. Record the strongest case against the favored
option. Obtain individual judgments before group discussion when proportionate,
then preserve material changes and dissent. This is our candidate safeguard;
we have not demonstrated that it prevents conformity or anchoring.

Use numbers where there is a defensible basis. Record the forecast target,
time horizon, evidence and uncertainty; leave unsupported quantities UNKNOWN.
If a probability or weight changes the preferred option, disclose that dependence.
Do not force unrelated harms into money or fabricate precise probabilities to
make a spreadsheet complete.

## Constitutional fit and costs

I–II concern authorized ends and decision rights; IV.1 supports intelligible
reasons, alternatives, uncertainty, provenance and dissent; IV.2 concerns actual
dependence and human ability to understand and reject a proposed course. III
separates judgment, execution and verification. These are mappings to the
[proposal](../../constitutional-design/drafts/CONSTITUTION_v0.1.md), not new grants.

More structure can expose errors but also slow legitimate work, overload
reviewers or allow whoever controls the option list to control the outcome.
Use depth proportionate to consequence, irreversibility and uncertainty.
Evaluate missed hazards and unnecessary delay together. The
[workflow](../../process/DECISION_MAKING.md) is the practical entry point.
