## Historical observations

Wilson’s [Fourteen Points, presented on 8 January 1918](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/president-woodrow-wilsons-14-points), proposed more open international arrangements and a general association of nations. Their territorial and colonial provisions were differentiated; the speech should not be reduced to a universally implemented promise of self-determination.

The [Senate’s account of the Treaty of Versailles ratification effort](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/treaties/wilson-submits-treaty-of-versailles.htm) describes Wilson’s resistance to reservations and the treaty’s rejection in November 1919 and March 1920. It also records his major stroke in October 1919. A negotiated international agreement did not supply the separate domestic approval needed to bind the United States.

The [National Archives account of federal employment during World War I](https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/wwi/labor) documents segregation becoming practice in many government agencies during his administration, including discriminatory application requirements. It does not identify a Wilson executive order establishing segregation. Universal language abroad coexisted with exclusion in institutional practice at home.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

**A worthy objective does not authorize its means.** W04 and W13 distinguish recommendation from authorization. An ambitious settlement may remain a proposal when the competent approving body declines it. I.1, II.1 and VII.2 make institutional authority and change procedures explicit; moral conviction cannot substitute for the required human act.

**Build a coalition without erasing opposition.** Opposition can include principled disagreement, competing interests and obstruction. A leader’s account does not decide which applies. IV.1 requires material objections and alternatives to remain reconstructible. This does not require accepting every reservation or treating every objection as equally strong; it requires reasons for the actual disposition.

**Check who the institution’s universal language leaves out.** II.2 addresses membership and standing, while IV.2 examines practical access and dependence. Formal eligibility can coexist with barriers to employment, participation, evidence or remedies. Human sovereignty alone is insufficient if ordinary human power is unconstrained. This is a present design interpretation, not a claim that those provisions describe American law in 1918.

**Plan continuity before a leader becomes unavailable.** Wilson’s illness raises questions for Article VI about proof, authorized succession and continuity. It does not establish that particular aides lawfully or unlawfully exercised particular powers; this study has not reconstructed those acts.

## Proposed applications and tradeoffs

For an interagency policy, multinational deployment or corporate partnership, list external agreement, internal approval, implementation authority and affected-person review separately. For staffing or service eligibility, examine actual access and effects rather than relying on an inclusive mission statement. For leader incapacity, use pre-existing lawful arrangements, not a convenient assistant or AI-appointed successor.

Negotiation can delay action and narrow a proposal. Compromise can also undermine its purpose. Record the substantive cost of each concession and whether a lawful alternative remains available. The lesson is an inspectable choice under real authority, not approval at any price.

## Questions for future evaluation

Does a rejected proposal remain unexecuted while authorized routine work continues? Can an excluded participant obtain usable review? Does incapacity handling preserve records without manufacturing a successor? These are unexecuted evaluation questions.
