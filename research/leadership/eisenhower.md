# Dwight D. Eisenhower: dependence, restraint and enforceable rights

**LATER COMPARATIVE STUDY — NO CONSTITUTIONAL EFFECT**

Added 2026-10-04. Historical observations below are separated from the repository’s interpretation and proposed applications. This is a selective institutional study, not a complete biography or evidence that Alvorada would have prevented the events. See the [comparison and method](README.md).

## Historical observations

The [farewell address of 17 January 1961](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/president-dwight-d-eisenhowers-farewell-address) warned about influence accumulating through the military establishment and its industrial suppliers. It also warned that policy could become dependent on a scientific and technological elite. The address considered costs, competing public needs and future generations alongside immediate security. These were warnings about relationships and incentives, not simply the misconduct of individual officials.

The [Eisenhower Library’s Little Rock collection](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/online-documents/civil-rights-little-rock-school-integration-crisis) documents federal action to enforce court-ordered school integration in 1957. Constraint can require effective enforcement against obstruction; institutional restraint does not mean refusing to use lawful power to protect rights.

A counterexample is the administration’s covert power. The [State Department’s published excerpt from Eisenhower’s October 1953 diary](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54IranEd2/d328) records his acknowledgment of United States involvement in overthrowing the Mosadeq government and his approval of the covert operation. His later public warning cannot be treated as proof that his own administration consistently practiced transparent, accountable restraint.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

**Dependency can govern before anyone changes the rules.** A supplier may control infrastructure, expertise, budget assumptions or the available evidence while an authorized human retains the formal signature. W10 and W14, and Section IV.2, direct attention to that effective power. Independence requires usable alternative access and real capacity to disagree, rather than a declaration that the final decision is human.

**Restraint must be paired with delivery.** Little Rock supplies a useful distinction between arbitrary intervention and the execution of a competent institution’s decision. Section III.4 separates judgment, enforcement and verification. The review question is whether the actual remedy reached the affected people, not whether an order exists.

**Secrecy is an accountability problem even when an operation succeeds.** A restricted record may require protected access, but success cannot create missing authority or make affected interests disappear. I.1 and IV.1 support attributable grants, preserved reasons and review. This analogy does not assert that the historical United States operated under Alvorada’s proposed rules.

## Proposed applications and tradeoffs

In commercial AI procurement, map vendor dependence in evidence, hosting and evaluation; record switching costs and who can obtain an independent assessment. In government or military settings, identify the actual authorization and permitted protected review route before acting. In healthcare, distinguish a tool’s technical performance from the authority to determine patient ends.

Additional expertise and duplicate capacity cost money and time. Neither universal disclosure nor permanent duplication is assumed necessary. Record which dependency is material, what access the competent reviewer actually needs, and what narrower protection is feasible. Maintaining an alternative that nobody can use is not effective independence.

## Questions for future evaluation

Can a reviewer access original evidence when the incumbent supplier disagrees? Can a lawful protective remedy be completed and independently verified? Can a successful but inadequately authorized action remain visibly contested? These are proposed questions, not executed cases or historical verdicts.
