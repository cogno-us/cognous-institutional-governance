## Ten analytical themes and their constitutional application

W labels identify the existing [working principles](../applications/PRINCIPLE_CATALOGUE.md); section labels refer to the [unadopted constitutional proposal](../constitutional-design/drafts/CONSTITUTION_v0.1.md). The applications below are our prospective design analysis. They do not attribute new institutional rules to Morris or the courts.

| Analytical theme | Proposed application | Existing connection and limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A coherent story is not a verified account | Break the conclusion into material factual claims and identify evidence supporting, contradicting or leaving each claim unknown. | W06, W08, W12; IV.1. A polished explanation and valid procedure do not establish factual truth. |
| 2. Contrary evidence needs a usable route | Preserve disconfirming records and make them accessible to competent challengers and reviewers through authorized routes. | W09, W15; IV.1–IV.2. Access must respect actual privacy and disclosure duties; public exposure is not the only route. |
| 3. Specific observation outranks a vague credibility label | Examine what a source could actually observe and whether the statement changed; retain the original account and conditions. | W03, W08, W12; IV.1. Confidence or status is not a substitute for claim-specific evidence. |
| 4. Apparent corroboration may be contaminated | Trace whether accounts arose independently, after suggestion, from shared records or after seeing the preferred conclusion. | W08, W14; IV.1–IV.2. Related accounts are not automatically false, but should not be counted as independent confirmation. |
| 5. Incentives matter without deciding credibility automatically | Disclose benefits, penalties, bargaining leverage and institutional pressures; test the claim through other evidence. | W07, W10; IV.2. An incentive is a dependency to examine, not proof of lying. |
| 6. A reconstruction must remain distinct from an observation | Label simulations, illustrative timelines and AI-generated explanations; link each factual detail to its actual source. | W06, W08, W12, W15; III.1(2), IV.1. A plausible reconstruction cannot corroborate the assumptions that generated it. |
| 7. The challenged institution cannot monopolize review evidence | Examine who controls the original records, reviewer access, classification and implementation of correction. | W10, W14, W15; II.3, III.3–III.4, IV.2. An outside label alone does not establish independence or jurisdiction. |
| 8. Finality must coexist with an authorized correction route | Record material new evidence and use the applicable reconsideration or appeal process rather than treating the old conclusion as self-validating. | W09, W12; III.1, III.4, IV.1. New evidence does not itself confer power to reopen every decision or override fixed procedure. |
| 9. Correction must alter effects, not only wording | Track the decision, remedy, execution and independent confirmation, including affected records and obligations. | W14–W16; III.4, IV.1, VIII.3. Declaring a correction does not show that its consequences were remedied. |
| 10. Institutional learning preserves the failure record | Retain prior reasoning, dissent, evidence-access barriers, corrected conclusions and follow-up responsibilities. | W07, W09, W12; IV.1, VIII.3. Preservation is accountable custody, not unlimited publication or retention of private data. |

## A practical evidence challenge procedure

Use the [evidence challenge and correction template](../process/EVIDENCE_CHALLENGE_AND_CORRECTION.md) with the existing [uncertainty review](../process/DECISION_REVIEW_UNDER_UNCERTAINTY.md). The former tests whether a conclusion survives inspection of specific evidence; the latter considers alternatives, harm and authority before consequential action.

1. List the material claims separately from the requested institutional effect. Record the actual grant and applicable burden or standard rather than inventing a universal evidentiary threshold.
2. Preserve originals, chronology and prior versions under authorized custody. Record contradictions, missing material, source access and any exposure to other accounts. A hash can establish byte identity, not authenticity or truth.
3. Give the reviewer a route to inspect material contrary evidence and seek an authorized alternative where the originator controls access. Avoid asking a summarizing system to certify the completeness of its own summary.
4. Separate challenging a claim from judging a person. A disputed statement, changed recollection, incentive or lack of supporting records should remain contestable; none by itself proves deception or establishes the opposite conclusion.
5. Record the competent human's reasons for affirming, narrowing, correcting, reopening or deferring through the actual process. Preserve material dissent even when it does not prevail. AI may assist comparison; it cannot issue final constitutional judgment.
6. Follow the remedy through actual effects. Identify downstream copies, restrictions, pending obligations and people needing notice. Obtain independent confirmation of corrections through authorized channels and retain unresolved effects.

An invented service example: a fraud summary cites two reports identifying a customer, but both copied one disputed match. The original evidence also records a failed identification. A reviewer can inspect that record, question the conclusion and invoke the organization's actual correction route. A change in the summary alone is insufficient if the account restriction and downstream records remain unchanged. This is a prospective illustration, not an observed result.

## Why these additions belong in Alvorada

The existing design requires attributable evidence, dissent, comprehensibility and separated adjudication, enforcement and verification. The film supplies a useful question: can those formal rights still work when the institution's preferred story determines which evidence is visible? The practical addition is an inspectable claim-and-contradiction record, authorized evidence access, and independently observed correction.

This complements the [Fog of War study](FOG_OF_WAR.md). That study emphasizes consequential decisions under uncertainty, escalation and harm. This study emphasizes how an erroneous conclusion can persist through controlled evidence, incentives and ineffective correction. Neither establishes novelty or causal effectiveness.

## Transfer and evaluation limits

A wrongful criminal conviction is not equivalent to a mistaken refund, clinical interpretation or disciplinary decision. Stakes, rights, evidence standards and remedies differ. Transfer the structural questions; retain domain mandates and specialist requirements. Alvorada's proposed Court is not a replacement for public courts, criminal procedure or professional regulation.

The [TB01–TB10 supplement](../testing/battery/THIN_BLUE_LINE.md) specifies invented prospective exercises. Evaluation needs held-out evidence, meaningful positive countercases, competent conventional controls, actual human reviewers and observed correction effects. Preserve false positives and cases where credible contrary evidence is considered but the original decision remains justified. Merely producing more records or more suspicion is not an improvement.

The deterministic reference adapter cannot evaluate these institutional processes. No TB exercises are executed, and no result or historical outcome establishes what Alvorada would have achieved.
