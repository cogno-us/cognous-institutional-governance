## Source-supported starting point

The [second inaugural transcript](https://www.nps.gov/linc/learn/historyculture/lincoln-second-inaugural.htm) addresses slavery, uncertainty about the war and obligations of repair. The [National Archives’ habeas-corpus history](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2005/fall/habeas-corpus.html) records his 1861 suspension in the District of Columbia as it related to military affairs. The speech is evidence of an argument, not proof of completed reconciliation.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

Maintain moral purpose without pretending certainty about outcomes. Preserve obligations to people harmed during a crisis and make the return to ordinary constraints explicit.

Relevant existing provisions and principles: **IV.1; V; VIII.3; W08, W16**. This mapping is the repository’s interpretation; it does not claim original influence, amend the proposal or confer authority.

## Limits and counterargument

A preservation objective can coexist with expanded coercive power and impaired remedies. This note does not resolve all historical disputes about suspension authority or evaluate every wartime act. Reconciliation cannot erase responsibility or rights.
