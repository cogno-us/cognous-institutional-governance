## Source-supported starting point

The [Stanford Encyclopedia’s Mill study](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mill/) examines liberty, individuality and protection from social as well as governmental coercion. This note uses a scholarly secondary account; it is not a close reading of all editions of On Liberty.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

Protect material disagreement and personal agency against organizational conformity. Treat an unpopular view as a claim to evaluate, not evidence of disloyalty. Challenge mechanisms must work when disagreement is socially costly.

Relevant existing provisions and principles: **II.2; IV.1; W07, W09**. This mapping is the repository’s interpretation; it does not claim original influence, amend the proposal or confer authority.

## Limits and counterargument

Discussion is not a duty to act on every claim. Distinguishing harm from offense, and coercion from legitimate constraints, needs substantive judgment. This lesson does not transfer Mill’s full political programme into Alvorada.
