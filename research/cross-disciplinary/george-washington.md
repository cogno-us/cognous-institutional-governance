## Source-supported starting point

The [Senate’s farewell-address history](https://www.senate.gov/about/traditions-symbols/washingtons-farewell-address.htm) describes his retirement and warnings about faction. [Mount Vernon’s slavery history](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/slavery/george-washington-and-slavery) documents his ownership of enslaved people. These institutional accounts support a selective comparison, not an assessment of his entire presidency.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

Design institutions that survive a founder’s departure. Separate the person, office, records and authority to appoint a successor. Preserve legitimate disagreement rather than interpreting every faction as disloyalty.

Relevant existing provisions and principles: **II.2–II.3; VI; W07, W12**. This mapping is the repository’s interpretation; it does not claim original influence, amend the proposal or confer authority.

## Limits and counterargument

Voluntary restraint by an exceptional founder is fragile. Retirement does not establish inclusive legitimacy; slavery is a substantive contradiction to a simple liberty narrative. Opposition and political organization are not inherently abuses.
