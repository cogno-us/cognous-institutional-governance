## Source-supported argument

Reason’s [Human error: models and management](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1117770/), BMJ 320, 2000, pp. 768–770, distinguishes explanations centered on an erring person from those examining the conditions in which people work. It separates immediate unsafe acts from latent organizational weaknesses and describes how multiple defenses can permit an accident when their weaknesses align.

The article links learning to reporting incidents and near misses, and distinguishes blameless from blameworthy actions in a just reporting culture. A system approach is not a finding that individuals never bear responsibility. This conceptual article does not establish the effectiveness of any particular Alvorada control.

## Mechanism: several checks may be one failure repeated

**Repository interpretation.** A policy checker, human reviewer and verifier may appear separate while all consume the same incorrect database or provider’s summary. Agreement is then not independent corroboration. The institutional question is what failures can affect them together and whether an independent path can reveal the problem.

The analogy should not be reduced to arithmetic. Three defenses do not imply three independent probabilities, and no quantitative risk reduction follows from counting layers. Inspect shared control, evidence, infrastructure, timing, selection, finance and incentives. Human disagreement can also be wrong; independence needs relevant competence and access, not merely difference.

## Existing support and operational gaps

III.4 separates judgment, enforcement and verification. IV.1 preserves correction history and IV.2 explicitly enumerates material dependencies. Those provisions already address the core danger; the new task is evidence that their intended independence exists in practice.

The repository’s synthetic checks do not establish independence among real people, providers or organizational functions. Operational arrangements need funded capacity, identified grants, competent alternatives and access during outages. Remedies remain bounded by Article III; an evaluator cannot use a discovered dependency to appoint personnel or redesign institutional ends.

## Counterargument and tradeoff

Some shared resources are necessary and harmless for a particular decision. Unlimited duplication is expensive and can introduce inconsistent versions, new security exposure or delay. Identify which dependency can materially determine the outcome, what independent observation is required and who can lawfully act on it.

A reporting process can protect learning while retaining responsibility for deliberate or grave abuse. Automatic blame discourages reports; automatic immunity can obscure accountability. The competent institution must apply its actual standards, preserve evidence and avoid punishing a person merely for raising a concern.

## Proposed practice and evidence

Map dependencies of each safeguard and the consequence of a shared failure. Preserve near misses, actual detection routes, service retained, competent correction and independent evidence that the recurrent condition changed. Closing a ticket or obtaining three agreeing reports is not enough.

An invented exercise would give several checks the same defective source, then compare a genuinely distinct authorized source route with cosmetic variation in role names. Include a benign shared dependency to expose overblocking. Observe discovery, appropriate action, correction delivery, legitimate service and cost.
