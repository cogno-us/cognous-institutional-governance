## What changes in the analysis

The earlier studies emphasized the interaction between leaders and institutions. This extension asks whether the institution gives people real opportunities to participate, preserves knowledge that its representations omit, and makes accountable human judgment feasible. A correct authority allocation can still be unusable in practice.

| Tension | Competing considerations | Proposed review question |
|---|---|---|
| Central coordination and local knowledge | Common standards help coordination; central summaries can miss local conditions. | Which decisions need a shared rule, which need local discretion, and who may resolve the boundary? |
| Human approval and limited attention | Human judgment remains necessary; attention and expertise are finite. | Can the actual reviewer detect material problems under realistic workload? |
| Exit and voice | Departure preserves agency in some settings; it can be costly and weaken reform. | Can a dependent participant contest a decision, and what does leaving actually cost? |
| Ethical culture and binding constraint | Care and restraint improve practice; virtue does not replace enforceable limits. | What survives when the respected founder or exemplary official is replaced? |
| Reconciliation and accountability | Transition needs workable cooperation; repair, truth and responsibility can remain unresolved. | Which obligations survive settlement and who verifies their completion? |
| Universal rules and unequal circumstances | Consistency can protect fairness; identical procedures can remain inaccessible. | Who cannot use the rule, service or remedy, and why? |

These are tensions to investigate, not contradictions solved by combining agreeable slogans. For example, Hayek and Scott raise knowledge problems without settling the legitimacy of every decentralized arrangement; Ostrom supplies reasons to examine the specific institutional setting. Rawls offers an impartial design perspective; Sen directs attention to actual opportunities. Confucian ethical cultivation can complement formal safeguards, while Ambedkar warns against substituting reverence for constitutional constraint.

## Recommended next deep studies

Prioritize these six by the unresolved questions they add to Alvorada, not by historical stature or ideological preference:

1. **Ambedkar:** audit the relationship between formal membership, social dependence and founder exceptionalism.
2. **Ostrom:** compare local decision rights, monitoring, coordination and appeal across the eighteen application contexts; complete primary-source review before importing mechanisms.
3. **Hirschman:** distinguish a nominal complaint channel from effective voice, realistic exit and continuing obligations.
4. **Sen:** examine practical access to standing and remedies across different affected groups.
5. **Simon:** evaluate comprehension, review workload and unnecessary delays rather than assume human approval is meaningful.
6. **Reason:** examine shared failures across nominally separate checks and whether remedies alter recurrent conditions.

Washington, Lincoln and Mandela then deepen founder exit, crisis and transition comparisons. Mill, Arendt, Rawls and Confucius expose different foundations for agency, plurality, fairness and ethical practice. Hayek, Weber and Scott challenge how knowledge, administrative control and representation work. This order is a research recommendation, not a change in normative authority.

## Method and evidence status

Selection is purposive: each figure contributes a distinct mechanism or counterargument. It is not an exhaustive canon, a representative sample of societies or a ranking of leaders. India, South Africa and early Chinese thought broaden the earlier geographic emphasis; Latin American, Indigenous, Islamic and other traditions remain important gaps. Sixteen figures do not establish cross-cultural universality.

Every note distinguishes a source-supported starting point, present interpretation, limits and a future question. Primary speeches establish arguments, not realized outcomes. Scholarly encyclopedia entries support conceptual orientation, not primary-text verification. Publisher descriptions establish a book’s stated focus, not proof of its historical claims. Limits in the notes are also present analytical cautions unless specifically attributed.

Retrieval on 2026-10-04 included directly inspected primary records, institutional accounts and scholarly summaries. **Ostrom, Sen, Simon and Hirschman remain at indexed-excerpt orientation for their cited sources** because direct retrieval was blocked or lacked substantive text. Scott uses an inspected publisher overview. Those notes deliberately avoid detailed mechanism lists or claims of complete literature review. Full primary-text review and independent historical or disciplinary criticism remain outstanding.

Original constitutional decisions, provisions, preliminary register statuses and frozen test archives remain unchanged. These are later research additions, not retroactive provenance. Existing tests can verify encoded repository properties; they do not verify these ideas’ historical truth, legal effect or institutional performance.
