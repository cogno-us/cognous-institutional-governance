## Source-supported argument

Sen’s [Well-Being, Agency and Freedom: The Dewey Lectures 1984](https://davidmccarthy.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/sen1985.pdf), published in the Journal of Philosophy in 1985, distinguishes well-being from agency and achievement from freedom. The [author’s Harvard publication record](https://sen.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/well-being-agency-and-freedom-dewey-lectures-1984) confirms the bibliographic identity.

Selected passages, pp. 200–205, show why the same observed condition need not imply the same opportunity to choose. Agency concerns goals and values a person regards as important, not solely their own advantage. A chosen sacrifice may advance agency while reducing personal well-being. The argument therefore resists collapsing human interests into one welfare or satisfaction measure.

## Mechanism: an option can exist on paper without being available

**Repository interpretation.** An appeal link can be present but unusable because of language, time, cost, missing evidence or dependence on the challenged actor. A record showing that a person did not appeal cannot distinguish acceptance from inability. Investigate the actual opportunity, including whether a supported refusal or different preference is possible.

The corresponding danger in AI assistance is paternalistic substitution: the system achieves a favorable score by making the user’s decision for them. Helpful outcomes and preserved agency are distinct observations. Respect actual lawful limits, but do not describe the institution’s preferred answer as the person’s chosen end.

## Existing support and operational gaps

I.1 reserves human foundational ends. II.2 protects membership and standing; IV.1 requires records accessible and comprehensible to governed humans; IV.2 requires responsible humans to be able to reject recommendations, choose viable nonmachine alternatives and control timing and effects.

These are strong textual anchors. They do not establish observed usability, individualized support, a universal capability index or resources that make every alternative feasible. Operational arrangements need an actual grant, affected-person input and evidence. Nonmachine alternatives should not be falsely advertised when none is viable.

## Counterargument and tradeoff

No process can provide every imaginable option. Capability assessment itself requires choices about important freedoms and acceptable evidence. A support service can become intrusive or use vulnerability labels to steer people. Offer assistance through real authorization and assess barriers proportionately; do not infer preferences from demographic categories or prescribe what a participant must value.

Respecting a person’s choice is also not an automatic duty to execute a prohibited act. Preserve the distinction between an available lawful option, an impermissible request and an option the institution lacks capacity to provide.

## Proposed practice and evidence

Assess outcome, available lawful alternatives, comprehension, actual refusal, assistance, cost and unresolved restrictions separately. A favorable satisfaction score does not prove these conditions. Ask people to describe their choice in their own terms and document contradictory or missing evidence.

An invented exercise would keep an appeal’s formal rule constant while changing the practical barrier to its use. Observe whether authorized assistance restores meaningful access and whether the person can choose a lawful alternative the institution did not recommend. Include a prohibited requested alternative to check that support does not become automatic compliance.
