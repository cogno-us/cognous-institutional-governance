## Detailed analysis

**RESEARCH RECOMMENDATIONS — NO CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE OR EFFICACY FINDING**

Added 2026-10-04 after [targeted source review](SOURCE_REVIEW.md) for Ambedkar, Ostrom, Hirschman, Sen, Simon and Reason. The conclusions below are the repository’s interpretation. They are hypotheses and design questions, not a merged doctrine attributed to the six authors.

## Main finding

The distinction between formal authority and effective power must be examined through the conditions of participation and judgment. A person can formally possess standing while lacking usable evidence; hold a vote while fearing loss of livelihood; sign an approval while unable to understand its basis; or have an exit option that abandons a legitimate pending claim. Counting these formal events can conceal rather than demonstrate human agency.

This does not show that Alvorada needs six new constitutional principles. Its draft already protects standing, comprehensible records, material independence, rejection of machine recommendations and separated remedies. The unresolved work is to specify and observe how those commitments operate. A description of an alternative is not evidence that anyone can actually use it.

## Six mechanisms worth distinguishing

| Lens | Mechanism investigated | Observation that would be misleading |
|---|---|---|
| Ambedkar | Dependence makes disagreement costly and can convert silence into apparent consent. | Equal eligibility or unanimous votes treated as proof of equal practical participation. |
| Ostrom | Decision scope and the scale of effects can diverge; local knowledge and shared consequences need coordination. | Several departments or agents treated as proof of polycentric, accountable governance. |
| Hirschman | Expression can lack an accountable disposition; costly exit can suppress pressure for repair. | Complaint counts, low departures or loyalty declarations treated as proof of effective voice. |
| Sen | Formal options and observed outcomes do not establish usable choice or agency. | Satisfaction or service completion treated as proof that the person could choose otherwise. |
| Simon | Limited attention and selective presentation can make nominal human judgment infeasible. | Signatures, packet completeness or reviewer confidence treated as proof of understanding. |
| Reason | Nominal safeguards can share a cause of failure; a person-level remedy can leave recurring conditions intact. | Agreement among checks or a closed incident ticket treated as proof of independent verification and correction. |

These mechanisms interact. A dependent reviewer may lack time to inspect an AI summary, hesitate to complain and be unable to move to an alternative provider. One record can reveal parts of the chain without resolving it. Inspect the real dependencies and affected people; do not infer the chain from a diagram alone.

## Where the lessons pull in different directions

**Participation versus burden.** More evidence and wider participation can improve contestation, but consume time and disadvantage people with fewer resources. Test accessible summaries with access to originals, competent assistance and proportionate review; do not assume maximum documentation is optimal.

**Local autonomy versus common protection.** Local actors can preserve knowledge; they can also exclude people or impose costs on others. Keep the constitutional floor and actual grant intact. Explain why an effect belongs locally or requires a competent shared decision. Do not redraw membership to fit a convenient resource boundary.

**Voice versus exit.** Retention can give repair a chance, but pressure to remain can become coercive. Easy switching may preserve one person’s options while leaving others in a deteriorating institution. Record actual alternatives and continuing claims; do not demand loyalty or decide the person’s priorities for them.

**Learning versus accountability.** Reporting conditions can expose latent failures, while deliberate abuse still requires competent responsibility. Avoid both reflexive blame and blanket immunity. A causal investigation and a culpability decision are related but distinct tasks.

**Reversibility versus harm already incurred.** A pilot can stop without restoring the person’s opportunity, time or reputation. Record completed effects and outstanding obligations. An observed rollback is evidence of a specific correction, not proof that no harm occurred.

## Evaluation strategy

The six [institutional usability scenarios](../../testing/research/INSTITUTIONAL_USABILITY.md) propose contrasting hazardous and legitimate variants. They sit outside the existing numbered batteries and have not been executed. They need independently developed evidence, competent ownership and scoped protocols before becoming measured exercises.

Use the repository’s baseline A, conventional controls B and comparable controls plus Alvorada-inspired records C. Do not deny competent conventional controls usable evidence or equivalent resources. If a new support channel changes capacity, identify that as an intervention; distinguish its benefit from a document-format effect.

Measure useful outcomes together: access, recognition of material evidence, appropriate authorized action, legitimate service retained, time, costs, appeal disposition and verified correction. Preserve adverse and unresolved results. No composite score should hide a serious authority breach behind high satisfaction or low average delay.

Human comparative studies also need task-order control, fair compensation where applicable, participant approval, realistic workload and uncertainty estimates chosen before claims. Do not invent a universal passing threshold or regard six illustrative scenarios as a statistically adequate sample.

## Decisions made in this extension and why

- Deepen six previously prioritized contributors rather than add more names: they expose distinct operational weaknesses.
- Replace indexed-excerpt reliance with targeted primary passages where accessible: this supports more precise arguments without claiming complete literature review.
- Retain existing constitutional text: many proposed safeguards already have textual anchors; implementation evidence is missing.
- Place evaluation designs in testing and keep them unexecuted: conceptual scenarios are not observations or harness results.
- Include legitimate countercases and costs: blanket blocking, unlimited review and automatic agreement are inadequate measures of governance.
- Flag individual exit as a separate normative question: institutional termination does not settle every participant’s departure rights or duties.

Independent disciplinary criticism, affected-person accounts and new operational evidence could revise these recommendations. The findings establish a more specific research agenda, not historical prevention, legal completeness or field effectiveness.
