## Source-supported starting point

The [Stanford Encyclopedia’s Confucius study](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/confucius/) discusses ethical cultivation, ritual, social roles and the moral conduct expected of rulers. Early texts and later traditions differ; this secondary account does not establish one uniform Confucian doctrine.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

Formal roles need practiced habits of care, responsibility and self-restraint. Examine what people routinely reward, tolerate and model, not just what the policy states.

Relevant existing provisions and principles: **II.1–II.2; IV.1; W07, W14**. This mapping is the repository’s interpretation; it does not claim original influence, amend the proposal or confer authority.

## Limits and counterargument

Cultivated virtue cannot substitute for enforceable constraints. Role obligations and hierarchy can protect continuity but can also suppress a subordinate’s agency. The useful comparison complements explicit standing and review; it does not justify paternal authority or transfer historical hierarchy into membership rules.
