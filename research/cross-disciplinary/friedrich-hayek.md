## Source-supported starting point

The [1945 essay The Use of Knowledge in Society](https://www.econlib.org/library/Essays/hykKnw.html), reproduced with permission by Econlib, argues that economically relevant knowledge is dispersed and includes circumstances of time and place. This is a primary text, not empirical proof of every market arrangement.

## Interpretation for Alvorada

Preserve routes for local knowledge to change a consequential decision. A centralized model or dashboard may lack information held by staff, patients, customers or residents. Record what aggregation leaves out.

## Limits and counterargument

Dispersed knowledge is not an argument against every central rule or for treating prices as complete measures of welfare. Coordination, externalities and rights remain substantive questions. Local knowledge may also be wrong or strategically withheld.
