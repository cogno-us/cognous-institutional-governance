# What the research teaches

**Executive summary:** preserve accountable human choices, make evidence easy
to inspect and put scarce review effort where mistakes would matter most.
Formal approval, many records and a successful outcome are each insufficient
on their own. Alvorada's added practical value still needs testing.

## Six findings in plain language

| Finding | Practical implication | Deeper evidence and analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Power can move without a rule changing | Inspect appointments, funding, evidence access and repeated practice—not only titles | [Constitutional history](history/COMPARATIVE_CONSTITUTIONAL_HISTORY.md), [Bogdanor](history/VERNON_BOGDANOR.md), [Evans](history/RICHARD_J_EVANS.md) |
| People need usable choices and challenges | Provide understandable reasons, accessible representation, a safe objection route and a viable alternative | [Six-study synthesis](cross-disciplinary/DEEP_SYNTHESIS.md) |
| A convincing account can omit decisive evidence | Keep originals, contrary accounts, uncertainty and source relationships inspectable | [The Thin Blue Line](films/THIN_BLUE_LINE.md), [The Fog of War](films/FOG_OF_WAR.md) |
| Strong leaders and experts still need constraints | Separate actual authority from competence; preserve noninvolved review and practical independence | [Leadership studies](leadership/README.md), [comparative work](COMPARATIVE_WORKS.md) |
| Review has a cost and should fit the act | Use standing grants for routine classes; concentrate deeper judgment on serious effects and contested assumptions | [Decision research](decision-making/README.md), [governance critique](decision-making/GOVERNANCE_CRITIQUE.md) |
| Learning needs real outcomes | Verify correction, harm, access and total burden; authorize rule changes separately | [Causal evaluation](decision-making/CAUSAL_EVALUATION.md), [AI source review](AI_ASSISTED_GOVERNANCE_SOURCES.md) |

These rows are this repository's synthesis, not a shared doctrine endorsed by
all the authors. Source passages, competing considerations and access limits
remain in the linked studies.

## What changed in the framework

The lessons motivate a [shared charter and contextual profiles](../constitutional-design/modular/README.md),
[proportional decision procedures](../process/PROPORTIONAL_GOVERNANCE.md),
[AI preparation and institutional support](../applications/ai-assisted-institutional-governance.md)
and a [bounded learning cycle](../process/VIRTUOUS_RECURSION.md).
They do not retroactively amend the original Constitution or its human decisions.

British constitutional analysis is explicitly credited to **Vernon Bogdanor**
where his reviewed work is used. **Richard J. Evans** adds a later study of
institutional breakdown and consent. Both attributions are scoped to the
reviewed sources; neither author is claimed to support Alvorada.

## Disagreements and limits that should stay visible

Participation can improve legitimacy while increasing coordination burden.
Continuity can preserve knowledge while entrenching exclusion. Separate review
can resist capture while sharing the same decisive funding or evidence source.
AI can reduce preparation while increasing checking work and reliance. Faster
service and fuller records can each coexist with uncorrected harm.

History provides mechanisms to investigate, not guarantees of transferability.
Historical dictatorship is not an analogy for every commercial disagreement.
Practitioner advice, historical interpretation, experiments and self-report
surveys have different evidentiary force. The underlying studies are selective,
with verification limits stated individually.

## What has actually been demonstrated?

The repo preserves design choices, proposed practices and reproducible synthetic
mechanics. It has not established incremental human decision benefit, usable
full adoption or long-term institutional resilience. Read the
[current evidence map](../docs/EVIDENCE_STATUS.md), [coverage crosswalk](../testing/COVERAGE_MAP.md)
and [remediation ledger](../docs/REMEDIATION_STATUS.md) before making efficacy claims.
