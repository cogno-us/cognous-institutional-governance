# Working design principles

These principles guide research and design. They are not adopted
constitutional law.

1. Govern power rather than substrate.
2. Capability does not create authority.
3. Intelligence does not create authority.
4. Recommendation does not create authorization.
5. Delegation does not transfer foundational sovereignty.
6. Machine validation does not itself create legitimacy.
7. Human authority can itself require constitutional constraint.
8. Unknown and unresolved states should remain representable.
9. Material dissent should not disappear merely because a contrary decision
   prevails.
10. Formal authority and effective power may diverge.
11. Governance should be proportional to consequence and reliance.
12. Constitutional development requires provenance.
13. Artificial intelligence recommendations do not constitute human
    constitutional decisions.
14. Institutional functions should not silently collapse into one another.
15. Human constitutional supervision requires meaningful comprehensibility,
    not exhaustive inspection of every machine operation.
16. Local execution success is not durable institutional state.
17. A repository state transition is not considered durable until it is
    persisted and independently observable on the authoritative remote
    repository.
