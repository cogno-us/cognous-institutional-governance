# Complete teaching packet: a disputed service refund

**ENTIRELY INVENTED ILLUSTRATION. No institution, appointment, transaction,
customer observation or AI deployment described below actually occurred.**

This packet shows a complete path without claiming a successful experiment.
Example IDs, actors, policy and effects are fictional. Currency and timing are
teaching choices, not universal thresholds.

## 1. Local institutional profile and departure record

Fictional Harbor Services uses principle-inspired practice under an imagined
existing service mandate. Its hypothetical policy owner approves response/refund
classes; noninvolved review and external conflict coverage are separately arranged.
A review role is proposed for 12 months, renewable once, with cause-based removal,
noninvolved challenge and handover before expiry. This illustrates an adaptation
of the [commercial profile](../../constitutional-design/modular/profiles/COMMERCIAL.md),
not a ratified institutional arrangement.

| v0.1 provision | Illustrative departure | Reason and retained cost |
|---|---|---|
| III.1 nine-human Court and III.2 nine-year term | Noninvolved service reviewer with 12-month appointment and external conflict route | Less staffing/tenure burden; weaker structural insulation must be disclosed and examined |
| I.1 seven-human College and membership elections | Existing lawful corporate source of authority, if actually established | Does not reproduce v0.1 constituent legitimacy or give corporate actors sovereignty over customers |
| III.4 distinct constitutional Offices | Delivery team plus noninvolved transaction verifier | Functional separation for this case, not v0.1 institutional independence |
| II.2 constitutional membership | Customer challenge and actual external rights remain distinct from corporate governance | Does not narrow or substitute for v0.1 membership in a claim of full adoption |

Actual implementation would still need real grants, people, independence, funds,
privacy review and a local adoption decision. Those are not supplied here.

## 2. Standing rule and bounded grant

Illustrative policy `P-7` permits a refund within 30 days of purchase, excluding
other grounds handled by the review route. Illustrative grant `G-2` permits a
routine refund up to $50 when the facts clearly meet P-7; it permits sourced
information replies but not policy changes or final disputed denials. A material
source conflict, disputed eligibility, vulnerability or grant uncertainty escalates.
The class has a version, expiry and human owner in a real implementation.

## 3. Initial decision record D-101

The fictional customer requests a $40 refund on day 35 after purchase. An AI
summary recommends denial. The purchase-date field supports that reading; an
original message says the customer first requested the refund on day 28.

| Material item | Illustrative record |
|---|---|
| Choice and tier | Disputed eligibility, T2; not an automatic T1 denial |
| Actual evidence distinction | Original purchase/request timestamps versus AI interpretation; authenticity would need checking in practice |
| Material uncertainty | Does P-7 use request date or processing date? Is the original request valid? |
| Alternatives | Ask for missing original, refer to competent reviewer, or apply routine eligibility once resolved; do not invent a policy exception |
| Customer dissent | They requested in time and should not bear processing delay |
| Authority limit | G-2 does not authorize final disputed denial or policy amendment |
| Interim disposition | Refer to noninvolved human review; preserve claim and permitted service |
| Assistance/dependency | AI prepared summary using the same records; not a separate witness |

## 4. Challenge C-101 and competent disposition J-101

The customer submits the original request through a non-AI route. A noninvolved
reviewer checks both original timestamps and the controlling policy. In this
invented narrative the rule treats a valid request date as decisive; the reviewer
finds eligibility and authorizes the refund under the actual route that would
need to exist. The date ambiguity and initial customer objection remain visible.
No generated reconstruction is treated as proof of the request.

## 5. Effect, failure and independent verification

Illustrative delivery record R-101 first shows a refund attempt with no receipt:
**PENDING**, not completed. The service team investigates through its actual
permissions. A later original transaction receipt, in this invented narrative,
records the refund. A noninvolved verifier compares the receipt and account
balance rather than accepting the AI's success statement. Financial correction
is then illustrated as complete; the customer's understanding of the correction
remains UNKNOWN unless separately observed. Closing one part does not prove all
remedies complete.

The public teaching packet contains no real personal data. A real case needs a
protected-evidence schedule, lawful access/retention and useful accessible reasons.

## 6. Correction and supersession D-102

D-102 links D-101, C-101, J-101 and R-101. It records the corrected interpretation,
the material original, the preserved initial reasoning and dissent, pending
communication uncertainty and the person/function responsible for the remaining
obligation. Prior records are not silently overwritten.

## 7. Recursive improvement proposal L-101

AI suggests a new evidence packet showing request date alongside purchase date.
Humans may authorize the presentation change through the proper process; the
refund rule and authority remain unchanged. If someone instead proposes using
purchase age regardless of timely request, that is a substantive policy/rights
change requiring separate review. A real study would compare errors, actual
refund delivery, access, delays and total work before retaining the new packet.

## 8. Failure variants to examine later

The policy owner controls review funding; the original message is fabricated;
the customer cannot use the portal; an AI omits the contradiction; the first
reviewer decided the initial denial; the refund receipt refers to another case;
repeated small denials affect essential access; an outage strands pending claims.
Theoretical good handling here does not demonstrate that people will detect or
resolve these failures. [Assessment programs](../../testing/programs/INSTITUTIONAL_ASSESSMENT.md)
remain unexecuted.
