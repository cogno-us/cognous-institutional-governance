# Institutional scope: governance with or without AI

**EXPLANATORY FRAMEWORK SCOPE — NO CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT**

Clarified 2026-10-04 at the maintainer’s direction. The public title is now **Constitutional Governance for Institutions (Alvorada)**. It is a research framework for broad institutional governance; AI governance is one application. The research name Alvorada remains. Following the owner's move on 2026-10-04, the canonical repository is
[cogno-us/cognous-institutional-governance](https://github.com/cogno-us/cognous-institutional-governance).

## What the framework examines

Its practical purpose is institutional decision-making. The
[decision workflow](../process/DECISION_MAKING.md) connects choices, objectives,
evidence and alternatives with actual authority, challenge and correction.
The [business and econometrics review](../research/decision-making/README.md)
adds candidate practices and effect-evaluation methods with explicit evidence
limits. Decision support requires no AI or numerical optimization.

Authority creation and limits; membership and standing; separation of functions; evidence, reasons and dissent; effective power and dependence; emergency; succession; change; transition and continuing obligations. A committee, respected founder, professional expert or records office can concentrate power without any AI present.

| Setting | Human-only example | Questions that remain relevant |
|---|---|---|
| Business or service organization | A manager rejects a complaint and controls the appeal evidence. | Actual authority, usable challenge, conflicting interests, delivered remedy. |
| Public body | A benefits office follows a uniform rule that some eligible people cannot use. | Mandate, standing, practical access, accountable review. |
| Nonprofit or community institution | A donor or founder influences allocation and suppresses objections. | Beneficiaries, dependence, dissent, permissible constraints and continuing obligations. |
| Education or research | A panel controls both an assessment and its appeal. | Expertise versus authority, separate judgment, source access and correction. |
| Cooperative or membership body | A committee changes participation rules while administering its own election. | Membership, selection independence, cumulative change and competent approval. |
| Emergency or transition | An unavailable officeholder’s aide continues work and claims additional powers. | Prior grant, bounded continuity, lawful succession and preserved obligations. |

These are invented reading examples, not reported incidents or findings that the framework would have prevented harm. The [eighteen domain guides](../applications/README.md) retain more detailed contexts, including specifically AI-related work.

## Technology is a participant or dependency, not the definition of governance

For a human-only workflow, examine human decision-makers, records, relationships, finance and incentives. For conventional software, also examine access controls, databases and operational routes. For AI-supported work, add model assistance and machine-dependence questions. No technology is required for the underlying distinction between formal authority and effective power.

A human’s expertise, popularity or indispensability also cannot create an otherwise missing grant. Conversely, using technology does not inherently make a decision illegitimate; evaluate the actual authority, effects and dependency.

## Scope versus constitutional identity

This clarification broadens public framing and the operational research agenda. It does not rewrite the origin in AI-collaboration experiments, change original decisions, waive the proposed Constitution’s AI-specific safeguards or amend its membership, offices, thresholds or protected ends. Earlier titles and dated source records remain in history; cite the title and commit actually consulted.

Three uses remain distinct: principle-inspired practice under existing lawful authority; separately authorized adaptation; and full constitutional adoption. A business, state, community or cooperative has its own real mandate and obligations. This repository does not supply those powers. Applying selected practices does not appoint Alvorada’s proposed Court or claim unchanged v0.1 adoption.

Broad relevance is a research claim about the questions addressed, not proof of universal transferability, legal completeness or measured performance across societies. The framework needs contextual assessment, affected-person input and evidence of costs as well as benefits.

## Begin with observable institutional conditions

The [institutional assessment program](../testing/programs/INSTITUTIONAL_ASSESSMENT.md) prioritizes usable access, feasible review workloads and independently verified remedies. Its first proposed workflow is complaints handled by people. AI-supported versions can be assessed later as separately identified conditions; their results should not be pooled without justification.

## Modular form and governance supported by AI

The [modular design proposal](../constitutional-design/modular/README.md)
separates shared protections from office terms and procedures suited to different
institutions. It preserves the fixed v0.1 proposal and records departures.
[AI institutional support](../applications/ai-assisted-institutional-governance.md)
addresses AI helping institutions govern, alongside governance of AI itself;
[virtuous recursion](../process/VIRTUOUS_RECURSION.md) explains authorized learning
from verified effects. All new benefits remain hypotheses pending assessment.
