# Adoption assessment: incentives, resistance and credible commitments

**PROPOSED PRACTICE — NO CONSENT, RATIFICATION OR EXECUTIVE COMMITMENT RECORDED**

Use before promising organizational adoption. The
[critique response](../research/decision-making/GOVERNANCE_CRITIQUE.md)
explains why staffing readiness is insufficient. Public research publication,
principle-inspired practice, separately authorized adaptation and full
constitutional adoption are different choices.

## Map who gains, pays and decides

| Actual party | Questions to answer from evidence |
|---|---|
| Executives and existing governing bodies | Which discretion changes? Which decisions remain theirs? What cost, exposure, continuity or information problem could binding review improve? What power or convenience would they lose? |
| Employees and operational teams | Who performs the extra work? Are time, training, alternates and protection for challenge actually available? Does speed improve or burden move downward? |
| Affected people and beneficiaries | Is notice understandable and challenge usable? Who is excluded, dependent or exposed to delay? What remedies would they actually receive? |
| Reviewers, executors and verifiers | Who selects, funds and can remove them? What evidence can they access? Can they make an adverse finding in practice? |
| Owners, funders and counterparties | Who can support or block the change under actual rules? Are conditions imposed on evidence access, appointments or publication? |

Record each party's stated position, known interests, uncertainty and evidence.
Do not assume executives share one incentive or that willingness to fund a pilot
constitutes consent by affected people.

## Build a conditional adoption case

1. Identify a concrete recurring problem, its observed baseline and the actual
   authority for addressing it.
2. Identify existing protections and competent conventional alternatives. Explain
   the proposed added benefit and burden as hypotheses, not a guaranteed payoff.
3. Map decision rights before and after: what is retained, constrained, delegated
   or separately reviewable, and through whose actual decision.
4. Record supporters, objections and potential blocking actions. Distinguish a
   scope or capacity concern from suspected protection of discretion; unknown
   motives remain unknown.
5. Obtain actual resource commitments: people, time, funding, usable evidence,
   notice, continuity and separate correction verification.
6. Test a bounded supporting practice where appropriate; assess costs and adverse
   findings. A limited pilot is not partial ratification of the Constitution.
7. The competent humans decide to continue, revise, defer, decline or begin the
   separate full-adoption process. Retain objections and pending obligations.

Potential incumbent benefits include clearer decision rights, more reliable
evidence, fewer unresolved disputes and continuity through leadership change.
These are our hypotheses. Costs include lost discretion, exposed errors,
resource commitments and limits on unilateral control. External pressure may
affect the bargain, but approval under pressure is not automatically legitimate
or evidence that all affected people consented.

## Make commitments observable

For each claimed commitment, record who can grant it, the actual instrument,
resources, duration, change/withdrawal route and evidence of delivery. Check who
can shut down review, suppress an adverse finding, remove a reviewer or deny
funding. Disclose dependence rather than labeling a sponsor independent.

Any proposed transparency, appointment or funding commitment needs competent
approval; this guide imposes none. Do not buy executive support by concealing
lost rights, immunizing misconduct or quietly narrowing membership. A sponsor
does not acquire authority over foundational ends by making the program viable.

## Blank adoption record

- Actual organization, problem, baseline and intended form of use: UNKNOWN
- Competent decision process, grant and affected membership/standing: UNKNOWN
- Before/after decision-rights map and material dependencies: UNKNOWN
- Party positions, stated interests, costs and disputed/unknown motives: UNKNOWN
- Actual support, objections, blockers and approved responses: UNKNOWN
- Resource and review commitments, evidence and withdrawal conditions: UNKNOWN
- Pilot measures, observed results, costs and independent assessment: UNKNOWN
- Actual decision, reasons, dissent and remaining obligations: UNKNOWN

The six unfilled Schedule O identities remain a specific proposal-readiness
blocker. Filling them would not establish consent, capacity, credible commitments
or institutional effectiveness. If those conditions cannot be established,
deferral or nonadoption is an acceptable outcome. Use the
[pilot guide](PILOT_EVALUATION.md) for principle-inspired assessment.
