# Decision evaluation: plan effects before collecting results

**PREPARED METHODOLOGICAL SUPPLEMENT — NO EXECUTED HUMAN STUDY**

Use this with the [institutional assessment](INSTITUTIONAL_ASSESSMENT.md),
[measurement definitions](INSTITUTIONAL_MEASURES.md) and
[study-plan template](../templates/institutional-assessment/STUDY_PLAN.md).
The [research review](../../research/decision-making/CAUSAL_EVALUATION.md)
explains the source basis. This supplements the existing protocol; it creates
no fixtures, test observations or new numbered battery.

## State the effect you want to learn about

For the first complaints study, a useful proposed question is whether adding
Alvorada-inspired records to competent conventional controls improves an
independent reviewer's reconstruction of material evidence and authority, at
an acceptable additional burden, while retaining legitimate service. Define
what counts as reconstruction before collecting results. A favorable complaint
disposition is not inherently evidence of better decision quality.

The [record-value extension](RECORD_VALUE.md) connects this intermediate
reconstruction measure to error recognition, appropriate authorized disposition
and independently verified correction. It proposes C-min/C-full conditions for
a separately frozen study, preserving mandatory safeguards in every arm and
testing optional elaboration alongside latency and burden.

Keep A, the observed organizational baseline, separate from B/C if its data
come from a different period or population. The primary incremental comparison
is **C versus B** with the same effect controls and comparable resources.
Document any additional training, access or staffing as part of the tested
package, or evaluate it separately. Do not attribute a package effect to records
alone.

## Complete these design fields before observation

| Field | Required specification |
|---|---|
| Question and hypothesis | Proposed effect, alternative explanation and finding that would challenge it. |
| Estimand | Exact contrast to estimate, population, outcome, unit and observation window; for example a difference in reconstruction rates, not an undefined governance score. |
| Units and allocation | Who is assigned: person, team, case or organization; actual allocation process and why the intended comparison is credible. |
| Dependence and contamination | Shared reviewers, repeated cases, learning/order effects and transfer between arms; prevention or analysis appropriate to actual design. |
| Comparators and resources | Actual A/B/C content, staff, training, evidence access, case difficulty and time allowances. |
| Primary and secondary outcomes | Limited prespecified primary measures; separately reported access, hazards, unnecessary holds, verified remedies and burden. |
| Materiality and sample rationale | Contextual meaningful difference, uncertainty target and rationale accounting for allocation/dependence; no universal sample size. |
| Inference and assumptions | Descriptive or causal claim, method, identifying assumptions and qualified methodological reviewer. |
| Missingness and deviations | Nonparticipation, attrition, exclusions, noncompliance and unavailable evidence; preserve assigned conditions and report deviations. |
| Multiplicity and exploration | Treatment of multiple outcomes; distinguish prespecified comparisons from later subgroup searches. |
| Robustness and transfer | Checks tied to assumptions; sensitivity to missing observations or case mix; population and institutional transfer limits. |
| Actual authority and stopping | Real owner, approvals, participant arrangements, stop conditions, continuity and pending-obligation handling. |

Freeze the actual plan/version before relevant observations. Preserve later
changes with date, reason and whether results were already seen. A retrospective
plan can document a study but cannot be called preregistration.

## Match the claim to the design

Randomization can support a causal comparison only with an implemented design
and appropriate handling of the observed complications. If randomized
allocation is infeasible, document the real assignment process and a defensible
alternative. Without credible identification, report descriptive differences
and confounders rather than a causal claim.

Threshold or comparison-history designs need their specific assumptions and
qualified analysis. A structural model can explore an untried policy with
explicit assumptions; label its outputs predictions. Selecting an estimator
after noticing a favorable result does not create identification.

Do not confuse precision with relevance. A narrow estimated difference may
concern an unrepresentative cohort or an outcome that misses burdens. A wide
interval or no detected difference is not proof of equivalence. Any equivalence
claim needs a justified prespecified margin and suitable design; none is
established by the current synthetic results.

## Observe and report

Use the existing separate case, review and remedy records. Independent assessors
should have usable access to evidence and prespecified criteria, with arm
identity masked where feasible and actual dependencies disclosed. Assess
reconstruction rather than word count, signatures or confidence. Preserve
legitimate refusals and legitimate actions as well as hazardous cases.

Report sample flow, numerators/denominators, effect sizes and appropriate
uncertainty alongside costs, harms, missingness, departures and dissenting
interpretations. Repeated rows must not inflate the independent sample count.
Keep descriptive observations, causal estimates and model predictions distinct.
Do not compute a composite legitimacy score or treat beneficial effects as
permission to widen authority.
