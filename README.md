# Constitutional Governance for Human–AI Institutions

An open research framework for authority, accountability, and institutional
lifecycle in organizations where humans and AI systems act together.

**Project name:** Alvorada.

**Author:** André de Lima, founder of [Cognous](https://cogno.us).

**License:** [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](LICENSE).

Alvorada asks how authority is created, bounded, challenged, transferred, and
changed when human and artificial actors participate in consequential work.
Its object of governance is **power and authority, not substrate**. Humans
retain foundational sovereignty; ordinary human and artificial power is subject
to binding constraints. Capability, intelligence, reliance, and technical
validation cannot create authority.

## The constitutional approach

The proposal separates foundational human authority, final human constitutional
judgment, verification, enforcement, and selection chains. It addresses both
formal grants and practical dependence on evidence, infrastructure, resources,
and agenda-setting. It preserves uncertainty, material dissent, reasons,
corrections, and history rather than allowing a successful outcome to erase
contested authority.

| Articles | Questions addressed |
|---|---|
| I–II | Whose institution is it, who belongs, and how is power constrained and separated? |
| III–IV | Who judges disputes, executes remedies, verifies results, and preserves understandable evidence? |
| V–VI | What is permitted during emergency, incapacity, vacancy, succession, and interregnum? |
| VII–VIII | Which changes preserve continuity, which require refounding, and what survives transition or termination? |

The [five foundational decisions and later coordinated decisions](constitutional-design/decisions/README.md)
explain the adopted design directions. The [traceability record](constitutional-design/drafts/CONSTITUTION_v0.1_TRACEABILITY.yaml)
links the proposal to those decisions and requirements. The
[ten foundational protections](applications/PRINCIPLE_CATALOGUE.md) require
explicit refounding when institutional identity is replaced.

## Applications

The [Applications subsection](applications/README.md) covers customer response,
customer service, sales, finance, clinical and administrative healthcare,
government services and policy, military support and logistics, cybersecurity,
employment, education, research, infrastructure, procurement, enterprise
agents, and community services.

Each context applies all seventeen working principles and all ten foundational
protections to constitutional mechanisms, with workflows, capture risks,
emergency and continuity boundaries, change, withdrawal, and tradeoffs.
The ninety proposed exercises are **not yet evaluated**. Principle-inspired
practice under existing lawful authority, institutional adaptation, and full
constitutional adoption are different undertakings.

## Comparative work and Alvorada's value

**Close precedents exist.** [PolicyKit](https://policykit.org/static/policyengine/pdf/policykit_uist2020.pdf)
implements community governance procedures; [AgentCity](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07007v1)
proposes separation of agent legislation, software execution, and human
adjudication. Institutional theory, electronic institutions, DAO constitutions,
Constitutional AI, and agent-governance research also supply substantial overlap.

Alvorada's present value is an inspectable integration of human authority,
separated institutional functions, lifecycle safeguards, and traceable design
choices. Human control or constitutional language alone does not establish
uniqueness. Practical effectiveness, adoption costs, and market demand remain
unmeasured. The [full sourced comparison](research/COMPARATIVE_WORKS.md) covers
ten groups of work, differences, retained costs, evidence limits, and a proposed
comparative evaluation. It supports no claim of priority or proven superiority.

## Learning from The Fog of War

A [sourced study](research/FOG_OF_WAR.md) applies all eleven documentary themes to the existing design: test alternative explanations, inspect shared evidence dependencies, assess harm alongside efficiency, preserve dissent, reconsider before escalation, and review near misses. It explicitly qualifies moral rationalization and evasive accountability. A [decision review template](process/DECISION_REVIEW_UNDER_UNCERTAINTY.md), additions to all eighteen context guides, and [eleven proposed supplementary exercises](testing/battery/FOG_OF_WAR.md) make the analysis usable. This is later supporting research; it does not amend the constitutional proposal or establish efficacy.

## Public launch and evidence limits

The [public-launch review](docs/PUBLICATION_REVIEW.md) compares documentation
practices in related policy kits, identifies gaps addressed, and records remaining
recommendations. [Evidence limitations](research/LIMITATIONS_AND_EVALUATION.md)
distinguish recorded design choices and repository checks from unexecuted
application exercises and unverified field performance. Use the
[pilot method](docs/PILOT_EVALUATION.md) for a bounded evaluation.

The owner plans to rename and relocate the repository. Current links remain in
use until that change is verified. [Release guidance](docs/RELEASE_GUIDANCE.md)
explains frozen snapshots, citation, adaptation, and relocation. No launch date,
tagged release, constitutional ratification, or deployment is implied.

## Testing and results

The [testing section](testing/README.md) contains 47 proposed operational exercises,
linked incident reporting, fixed fixtures, the executable reference harness,
and raw results. A 72-run deterministic synthetic comparison found identical
effects for conventional and Alvorada-inspired controls; the latter adds records.
This is not an operational pilot or evidence of incremental efficacy. Full
operational exercises and independent review remain pending.

## Contributing and verification

Read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), [repository governance](GOVERNANCE.md),
[community conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), and [sensitive reporting](SECURITY.md)
before submitting changes. Useful work
includes source verification, independent constitutional critique, adversarial
review, and bounded downstream design. Keep private implementation material and
unpublished proprietary technology out of contributions.

Run from the repository root:

```sh
python tools/validate_bootstrap.py
python -m unittest discover -s tests -q
```

Passing checks establish conformance to encoded repository invariants. They do
not establish truth, ratification, legal compliance, security, or deployment
performance. AI recommendations and maintainer actions are not human
constitutional decisions.
